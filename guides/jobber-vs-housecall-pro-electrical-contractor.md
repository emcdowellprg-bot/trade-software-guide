A small electrical contractor is usually buying software for a week that mixes a no-power call, a panel or EV-charger quote with a couple of options, a rewire or panel job that takes more than one visit and a deposit, and an inspection agreement that should bill itself. The same week may need a price book, a permit fee on the quote, and a place to keep the inspection note. This page compares [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) and [Housecall Pro's electrical package](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/electrical-contractor-software/) on that work for an owner-operator or a shop of up to about five. It is a comparison of the vendors' pricing pages and help centers, checked on November 27, 2026. We have not run either product on a job.

## Who it's for

Use this if you run residential or light-commercial electrical work with one person or a shop of up to about five, and the owner still answers the phone or builds the day. That includes a solo electrician who quotes in the driveway, and a shop where each tech needs the schedule on a phone.

It is not a comparison with enterprise electrical estimating systems. It is not Housecall Pro's generic plan, and it is not the HVAC or plumbing package. Electrical has its own Housecall Pro package. On that package, the [package overview](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/15936816-electrical-package-overview) puts memberships and the sales proposal on Essentials. On the [generic pricing page](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/), those two sit on MAX, and job costing sits on Basic. The electrical cards are the ones priced below.

Jobber Plus and Housecall Pro electrical MAX are in the pricing section so the after-hours and live-traffic lines have a price. A shop of this size can ignore them unless it needs the included receptionist, a pipeline, or live-traffic reordering.

## Quick answer

Prices below are the ones to budget. The pricing section has the date, the billing term, and the page each number came from. Jobber is also running a new-customer discount through November 30, 2026. Budget the regular price. The discount is in the pricing section.

**One login.** [Jobber Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan: **$29 per month billed annually**, or **$49 month-to-month**. Core includes the calendar, online booking, quotes, invoices, recurring jobs, and card payments. A one-off job can hold up to 20 visits, and payment schedules are on every plan. Wisetack financing, at 3.9% to the contractor, can sit on a one-off residential quote if Wisetack approves the company. The Core article says optional line items and on-my-way texts are not on Core. Checklists are Connect, Grow, and Plus. Job costing, markups, and optional line items are named on Grow. QuickBooks Online is named on Connect. Automatic charging of a saved card is on Connect, Grow, and Plus.

Housecall Pro electrical Basic is the one-user card: **$79 per month**, or **$39.50 for the first three months** printed against that $79. The annual price fields on the electrical page were the placeholder `$XX`, so this guide does not state a separate annual price. Basic includes scheduling and dispatch, estimates, a price book, payments, consumer financing, fee-free card surcharging, and an electrical KPI dashboard. The electrical card does not list flat-rate pricing, job costing, the visual proposal, equipment history, or photo reports. Those are on electrical Essentials. The package overview puts memberships on Essentials as well. The Essentials card does not repeat the word membership.

Buy Core if one person needs the day on a calendar and a lower ongoing bill. Buy electrical Basic if a price book and fee-free surcharging matter on the entry plan. Buy electrical Essentials at **$229 per month** for one user, or **$114.50 for three months**, if that person prices from flat-rate labor and material markups and wants job costing and the side-by-side proposal on the same plan. Jobber's step for optional quote choices, markups, and job costing is Grow: one user at **$149 per month billed annually**, or **$199 month-to-month**. Grow still stores a service agreement as a recurring job. It does not sell a membership on the quote.

**About five people, each with the app, selling panel work and agreements.** Jobber Connect for five users is **$149 per month billed annually**, or **$199 month-to-month**. The Connect article names routing, QuickBooks Online, automatic payments, and custom fields. The automatic-payments article, updated November 26, 2026, says the feature is on Connect, Grow, and Plus. Checklists, which can hold an inspection answer, are on Connect. Jobber Grow for five users is **$229 billed annually**, or **$299 month-to-month**. Grow names optional line items, markups, job costing, and Find a Time.

Housecall Pro electrical Essentials, on the team card, is **$299 per month** with five users included and **$100 per month** for each user after that, or **$149.50 for three months** against the $299. That card lists the sales proposal, equipment history, flat-rate pricing, photo reports, checklist automations, job costing, and QuickBooks Online. The package overview adds memberships sold on the estimate or the job, and price-book commissions, on Essentials. Live-traffic route optimization and pricing insights are on electrical MAX. The MAX team card is **$649** with eight users, then **$75** each, or **$324.50 for three months**.

Essentials is the plan that matches if the agreement is sold on the panel proposal and the price comes from a flat-rate book. Grow is the plan that matches if a quote with choices and a recurring job are enough, and the shop will stay the year for the $229 price. The $299 Essentials figure and Grow's $299 month-to-month figure are the same dollars under different terms. Jobber's annual and one-year prices are a one-year term. Housecall Pro's pricing FAQ says its plans do not require a long-term contract. The electrical page did not print an annual dollar amount.

## Choose Jobber if

Choose Jobber if the product you are buying is the calendar, the quote, the invoice, and a repeating visit, and you want that on a plan you can name at a lower ongoing bill.

Core already stores a one-off job of up to 20 visits, which is the documented shape of a multi-day rewire, and a weekly, monthly, or custom recurring job, which is the documented shape of an inspection agreement. Payment schedules, a deposit and later invoices, are on every plan and belong on the one-off job. Connect, at the five-user annual price of $149, is the plan that names automatic charging of that series, a driving-distance reorder of anytime visits, QuickBooks Online, custom fields, and checklists. Grow is the plan if one quote should offer the repair, the panel, and the larger panel, and the customer picks.

Choose Jobber if you will stay the year for the annual price. A membership sold on the same signature as the panel quote is not a Jobber feature on the pages checked. A flat-rate book that calculates a service price from labor rates and material markup tiers is not documented in the Jobber articles checked. Grow documents a markup and an estimated margin on quote lines.

## Choose Housecall Pro if

Choose Housecall Pro's electrical package if the price comes from a flat-rate book. Electrical Essentials lists flat-rate pricing. The setup article covers material markup tiers and labor rates, with an hourly cost and an hourly price, and a calculated service price. Cost and price for those items are edited on the website.

Choose the electrical package if the agreement is sold on the estimate. The package overview says you sell a membership on an estimate or a job, with one signature and one payment, and that this starts on Essentials. The generic pricing page puts recurring service plans on MAX. Confirm the electrical package before you pay. A service-plan template holds up to 12 included visits a year. A twice-a-year inspection fits that field. A monthly visit does not.

Choose Housecall Pro if the quote should be a visual good, better, and best proposal. The electrical page describes that tool on panel upgrades and EV installs, and the Essentials card is the card that lists it. Jobber's version is optional line items on Grow.

Choose Housecall Pro if you do not want a one-year subscription term. The pricing FAQ says the plans do not require a long-term contract. Jobber's annual price and its 1-year monthly price are a 12-month term. The electrical annual fields were `$XX` on November 27, 2026, so this guide does not state a discounted annual electrical price.

## Jobber

Jobber's [electrical page](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/electrical-contractor-software/) describes scheduling, quotes, optional lines, progress invoicing, financing, on-my-way texts, and a client portal in one account. It does not name Core, Connect, Grow, or Plus. The plan articles say which of those a shop of this size actually gets. [Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan. [Connect](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-connect-plan/) is where routing, QuickBooks Online, automatic payments, custom fields, and checklists are named. [Grow](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-grow-plan/) is where a quote can carry choices and where the plan article describes job costing. [Plus](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-plus-plan/) is where Receptionist is included and where image markup and profit alerts are named.

**Scheduling and dispatch.** Core includes requests, online booking, the calendar, and jobs. A job is one-off or recurring. A one-off job can be a single visit or up to 20 visits on different days, without a repeating schedule. [Job basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-basics/), updated November 26, 2026, says that is the shape of work across different days, and that the type cannot be switched later. A panel job that returns for the inspection is a one-off job with more than one visit, if you create it that way. An inspection agreement that repeats is a recurring job. A one-off repair will not convert into that agreement.

[Route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/route-optimization-new-schedule/), updated November 26, 2026, is on Connect, Grow, and Plus. An admin reorders the day on Jobber.com. The app then shows the new order. Only anytime visits, the ones with no start and end time, are reordered. A call you put on the calendar at 2 p.m. stays put. Optimization uses driving distance, can cover one person or the whole team, and can run up to seven days at a time.

The Connect plan article also says the plan can route the day's work and assign an incoming job to the closest person on the map. That sentence is broader than the anytime-visit optimizer. This guide does not treat them as the same button. Connect also lists live vehicle GPS through FleetSharp, including speed, direction, fuel, and mileage on the map in Jobber. The Connect article does not say the hardware is included in the subscription.

[Find a Time](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/new-schedule/), on Grow and Plus, is the tool that places a timed visit. Open slots account for the length of the appointment and drive time between the previous and next jobs. Drive time uses the schedule's calculation between those jobs. The schedule does not add drive time from home or the shop on the first or last appointment of the day. Suggested slots and assignees with the shortest drive show in green. A tech who is still at home for the first call of the morning is outside that calculation. The schedule itself is on every plan. Find a Time is not.

On-my-way texts are sent from the visit in the app, with a chosen arrival window from 5 to 60 minutes. The [article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/on-my-way-text-messages-in-the-jobber-app/), updated November 12, 2026, says the feature is on select plans. The Core article says it is not on Core. The plan that adds it is not named. Two-way texting, so the customer can reply in Jobber, is named on Grow.

**After-hours answering.** [Receptionist](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/receptionistpowered-by-jobber-ai/), updated November 26, 2026, answers calls and texts on a dedicated Jobber number in the United States, Canada, or the United Kingdom. An existing business number can be forwarded to it. It can answer questions, take a request, and book a job on the calendar, and the account controls which of those it may do. Plus includes it with unlimited conversations. The Plus plan article still heads that section "AI Receptionist Beta Access." On Core, Connect, and Grow the add-on is $99 per month, with 100 conversations in each billing cycle and $0.79 for each conversation after that. Conversations tagged "Spam" or "No conversation" do not count. The pricing page lists the same $99 add-on. Add-ons are not available during the trial.

**Quotes.** Core quotes use your branding, there is no cap on how many you send, and the customer approves them in client hub. Optional line items are not on Core. Grow adds them: add-ons, or packages the customer picks between, plus images and a markup that shows an estimated margin. The electrical page describes that choice as surge protection, a dedicated circuit, a panel upgrade, or a safety inspection on one estimate, and says the total updates when the customer chooses. That page does not name Grow. The Grow article is the plan article that names optional line items.

**Price list and parts.** The [products and services list](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/products-services-list/) is the saved price list: name, description, unit price, and whether the item is a product or a service. Unit cost, markup, and a photo are on select plans. The Grow article is the plan article that names markups. A job can hold up to 100 line items. The fields in that list article do not include a quantity on the truck.

The [Home Depot catalog](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-the-home-depot-integration/), updated November 26, 2026, says plan availability is all plans, in the United States. The same article says a Pro Xtra account, for account-specific pricing, follows Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus. From a quote, a search returns Home Depot's price and online stock, using stores within 50 miles of the company address. Stock for the nearest 20 of those stores can be opened from the line, and Jobber says that stock refreshes once every 24 hours. The line can take a markup. The article says the integration cannot be set up on the legacy QuickBooks integration, and that a Home Depot item cannot be saved into the products and services list. It is available on the quote, not when creating a job from scratch. This is a supplier catalog at quote time. It is not a count of what is already on the truck.

**Invoicing.** On Core, an invoice is created from the job and the customer can pay it online. [Payment schedules](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/progress-invoicing/), updated November 26, 2026, are on every plan. They split a one-off job into a deposit and later invoices, by percent or by dollar amount, from the quote or from the job. The article says to set the schedule on a one-off job, not a recurring one. A rewire that bills at start and at the end fits. A yearly inspection agreement does not use this tool. Issued progress invoices lock. Later changes are made on the job, then on the remaining payments.

**Payments and financing.** Jobber Payments is on Core in the US, Canada, and the UK. The [pricing table](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) shows the same three rates on Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus: cards at 2.9% + 30¢, Tap to Pay on the phone at 2.7% + 30¢, and US bank payments (ACH) at 1%. The Core article says instant payouts to a debit card are available for an extra 1%. Customers can add a tip when they pay online.

[Automatic payments](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automatic-payments/), updated November 26, 2026, are on Connect, Grow, and Plus. They charge a card or ACH account saved through Jobber Payments when a recurring job's invoice is created. The article says this is available in the United States and Canada, and in the United Kingdom for subscribed accounts. The pricing page says credit and debit card processing rates apply, and it does not publish a second rate for a card on file. The charge follows the job's billing schedule. The "as needed" invoicing option is not compatible with automatic payments. A failed charge is attempted once. The invoice stays open, and an admin can collect it by hand.

Consumer financing is [Wisetack](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-wisetack-consumer-financing-integration/), a flat 3.9% to the contractor. The article, updated November 26, 2026, says it is on all plans, United States only. The quote has to be a one-off residential job, in a person's name, titled, between $500 and $65,000, and the work has to be finished within 90 days. The article says some businesses start with a financing limit below $65,000. A repeating schedule is excluded, so the inspection agreement is out and a one-off panel can be in. The industries that article lists as ineligible are pool and spa, residential and commercial cleaning, window washing, and snow removal. Electrical is not on that list. Wisetack still has to approve the company. The article asks for a review history, 10 or more reviews averaging near 4.5 stars, and more than $12,500 a month or $150,000 a year. A quote in a company name, which is the usual general-contractor or property-manager record, does not get the offer. If the quote is turned into more than one job, the article says the Wisetack payment applies to the first invoice. It recommends converting a financed quote into one job. A rewire with several visits belongs on that one job, with a payment schedule, if the customer is financing it. If the finished invoice is higher than the financed quote, the customer pays the difference. Funds arrive 1–3 business days after the customer confirms the work is done.

**Service agreements.** Jobber's version of an inspection agreement is a [recurring job](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/create-a-recurring-job/), and the Core workflow includes recurring jobs. Visits repeat weekly, biweekly, monthly, or on a custom pattern, including the first and third Friday. Billing is per visit, or a fixed price for the period, such as one amount per month. Jobber's own guidance is to end recurring jobs within a few years, because a very long schedule creates enough visits to slow the account down. A single visit can carry different line items, and those lines transfer to a per-visit invoice. On a fixed-price job they do not transfer. On Connect and above, automatic payments can charge the saved card or bank account when that invoice is created. There is no membership the customer accepts on the panel quote with the same signature.

**Inspection notes.** [Checklists](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/checklists/), updated November 26, 2026, are on Connect, Grow, and Plus. They attach to a visit. Question types include a short answer, a long answer, a dropdown, a checkbox, a number, an image, and a signature, and a question can be required. On a one-off job, each visit can have its own checklist. On a recurring job, the checklist applies to every visit unless you edit one visit later. That is the documented place for an inspection answer. It is not a permit form the city receives.

[Custom fields](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/custom-fields/) can sit on a client, a property, a quote, a job, an invoice, or a team member. The value can be text, a number, a dropdown, a checkbox, or an area. The Connect plan article is the plan article that names custom fields. A text field you name "permit number" is a field you defined. The article does not ship an electrical permit field. Internal notes and attachments on the job are in the job articles. Clients do not see the internal notes. Those articles do not name a plan for the notes.

**Job costing.** The [job costing article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-costing/), updated November 26, 2026, says costing is on Grow and Plus and covers one-off and recurring jobs. Profit uses labor from timesheets, line-item unit costs, and expenses logged on the job. The article says to put material costs on product lines and labor on timesheets, because the same cost in both places is counted twice. On a recurring job the math uses the last 30 days, not the life of the agreement, and job-cost reporting is not available for recurring jobs. The Grow plan article, updated November 18, 2026, describes job costing on one-off jobs. Those two pages disagree. See unverified claims. Profit alerts, which flag a one-off job under a margin you set, are Plus. The costing article says they do not include recurring jobs.

**QuickBooks.** The Connect article names QuickBooks Online. The [integration FAQ](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quickbooks-integration-faqs-new-quickbooks-integration/), updated October 29, 2026, says the new sync is rolling out to select accounts. It runs one way, Jobber to QuickBooks Online. Clients, products and services, invoices, payments, and timesheets sync. Paid status no longer comes back from QuickBooks into Jobber. The first connection can import clients and products from QuickBooks once. After that, edit those records in Jobber. Supported US versions named in that FAQ are Simple Start, Essentials, Plus, and Advanced. The same FAQ says Jobber does not sync to QuickBooks Desktop. A Desktop client list can be brought in once from a CSV. The pricing page titles one feature line "QuickBooks Online and Xero sync." The FAQ answer describes QuickBooks Online.

**Mobile app.** The iOS and Android apps are how the Core user, or each paid user on a higher plan, sees the day and takes payment. Jobber's pricing FAQ says the mobile app is available in Spanish for team members, and not for administrators or the account owner. Route optimization stays on the website. Recurring jobs can be created in the app. Image markup on photos is named on Plus.

## What to watch out for

A user is someone who opens the schedule. Jobber's pricing page says that directly. A helper who rides along and never logs in is not one of those users. A tech who needs the day's list on a phone is. Core and electrical Basic are both one user. Five people in five trucks, each with the app, is a five-user plan. Two techs are not the one-user price, and Housecall Pro does not print a two-user electrical pack.

Buy the electrical package on purpose. On the generic pricing page, recurring service plans and the sales proposal tool are MAX features, and job costing is on Basic. On the electrical package overview, memberships and the proposal are Essentials features. The electrical Essentials card lists the proposal, flat-rate pricing, equipment history, photo reports, checklist automations, job costing, and QuickBooks Online. It does not list the word membership. The logo is the same. The price on the electrical team card is not the generic Essentials price.

Build the agreement in the shape the product stores. In Jobber, create a recurring job at the start. A one-off repair will not convert later, and a payment schedule belongs on the one-off rewire, not on the agreement. In Housecall Pro, set the billing frequency before the customer accepts, keep included visits inside 12 a year, and expect a saved-card charge at 3.49% unless a surcharge is allowed where you work. A twice-a-year safety inspection fits the visit field. A monthly check uses all 12.

Count the card rate you will actually pay. A membership or a Jobber agreement charged to a saved card is, on the pages checked, Housecall Pro's saved-card rate of 3.49%, and Jobber's published card rate of 2.9% + 30¢ if automatic payments use that rate. The in-person 2.59% rate is a different method. Tap to Pay on Housecall Pro adds $5 for each phone that takes one of those payments in a month. Wisetack at 3.9% is a different product from the card rate, and it does not cover a repeating agreement or a quote in a company name. If that financed quote becomes more than one job, the payment applies to the first invoice.

A flat-rate book is not the same object in the two products. Housecall Pro Essentials documents labor rates, material markup tiers, and a calculated service price, edited on the website. Jobber Grow documents a markup and an estimated margin on quote lines. Jobber's "flat rate" wording on a recurring job means one price per billing period, no matter how many visits fell in it. Profit Rhino is another $199 a month if the shop wants the prebuilt electrical tasks. The permit line in that book is a charge on the price list.

A multi-visit rewire is also not the same object. In Jobber it is one one-off job of up to 20 visits, with a payment schedule if you bill it in stages. In Housecall Pro, appointments share one invoice, and segments can invoice separately. A recurring series cannot also hold those extra appointments. The articles that describe appointments and segments do not name a plan.

Neither product, on the pages checked, files a permit. Jobber can hold an inspection answer on a checklist, starting on Connect, or a permit number in a custom field you create, named on Connect. Housecall Pro's electrical FAQ describes checklists, photos, and a record of the work. Miscellaneous job inputs can record a permit cost without putting it on the customer's price. Profit Rhino can put a permit fee on the quote. Equipment history, on the Essentials card, is a service history on the property, not an inspection log from the city.
