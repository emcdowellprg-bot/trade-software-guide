A solo pressure washer is usually buying software for one driveway at a time: a quote for the house, the flatwork, or the roof, the wash itself, and a card or bank payment taken before the hose is reeled up. The same customer may be back every month or every quarter. This page compares [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/), and [FreshBooks](https://www.freshbooks.com/pricing) on that work. It is a comparison of the vendors' pricing pages and help centers, checked on November 20, 2026. We have not run any of these products on a job.

## Who it's for

Use this if you are the owner-operator. You quote, you wash, and you collect. A helper who rides along is in scope. A helper who needs their own login is a different price, and that price is in the pricing section.

It is not a buying guide for a multi-crew company, and it is not Housecall Pro's HVAC, plumbing, or electrical package. Pressure washing is on Housecall Pro's standard plans. The help center's industry-packages collection lists HVAC, electrical, and plumbing, and does not list pressure washing.

It is also not a comparison with a payments-only app, or with a quoting tool that stops at the bid. Housecall Pro documents a [ResponsiBid](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/4151977-responsibid-integration-overview) connection for window cleaning, pressure washing, and similar work. A bid scheduled there can create a job in Housecall Pro. ResponsiBid's own price is not on that page.

## Quick answer

Prices below are the ones to budget. The pricing section has the date, the billing term, and the page each number came from.

**One login, a calendar, a quote, an invoice, and a card taken on the phone.** [Jobber Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan: **$29 per month billed annually**, or **$49 month-to-month**. Core includes the calendar, online booking, recurring jobs, quotes, invoices, and online payments. The pricing table lists Tap to Pay at 2.7% + 30¢ on Core. Automatic charging of a saved card, and QuickBooks Online, are named on Connect.

**The same person, if monthly washes should charge a saved card, or QuickBooks Online has to stay in sync.** Jobber Connect for one user is **$99 per month billed annually**, or **$139 month-to-month**. The Connect article names automatic payments on recurring jobs and the QuickBooks Online integration. Connect supports up to five users, so a helper who needs a login fits on this plan. The card says additional users are $29 per month.

**Job costing and a price book on the entry plan, with the lower in-person card rate.** Housecall Pro Basic is the one-user card: **$59 per month billed annually**, or **$79 month-to-month**, with a three-month promotion at $39.50 printed against the $79. Basic includes scheduling, estimates, invoices, a price book, job costing, and payments. Tap to Pay is 2.59%, plus $5 in any month that phone takes a Tap to Pay payment. QuickBooks Online is on Essentials, not on Basic. The recurring-job article does not name a plan.

**The invoice and the books, and the customer pays a link.** FreshBooks Plus is the plan that fits a real customer list: **$43 per month**, or **$464.40 per year**. Plus allows 50 active and archived clients. Lite is $23 per month, or $248.40 per year, and it stops at 5 clients. A route of houses does not fit on Lite. FreshBooks documents estimates, invoices, deposits, and recurring invoices on Lite and up, and the iOS and Android apps can send them. It does not document a job calendar or Tap to Pay. Keying or scanning a card in person is the Advanced Payments add-on at $20 per month, and that charge is 3.5% + $0.30.

Buy Core if one person needs the day on a calendar and a card on the phone, at the lowest ongoing subscription. Buy Connect if that person also needs QuickBooks Online or an automatic charge on the repeat wash. Buy Housecall Pro Basic if job costing on the entry plan matters more than the lower subscription. Buy FreshBooks Plus if QuickBooks is being replaced by the invoicing app, and a schedule of driveways is still kept somewhere else.

## Jobber

Jobber's [pressure washing page](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/pressure-washing-software/) describes quotes, a schedule, an invoice sent from the property, and Tap to Pay. It does not name the plan. The plan articles do. [Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan. [Connect](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-connect-plan/) is where automatic payments, routing, and QuickBooks Online are named. [Grow](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-grow-plan/) is where a quote can offer add-ons.

**Scheduling.** Core includes requests, online booking, and the calendar. A request form can sit on a website or a social page, and online booking can land on the calendar. A job is one-off or recurring. The Connect article adds routing: route the day's work, and assign an incoming job to the closest person on the map. A solo operator who already knows the order of the driveways is on the Core calendar. Routing is the Connect feature.

**Quotes.** Core quotes use your branding. There is no cap on how many you send, and the customer can approve them online. Optional line items are unavailable on Core. Grow adds them: add-ons, or packages the customer picks between, plus images and a markup that shows an estimated margin. That is the pressure-washing version of "house wash, or house wash plus the driveway and a seal" on one quote. Line items come from the products and services list.

[Payment schedules](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/progress-invoicing/) are on every plan. On a quote you can collect a deposit, by percent or by a dollar amount, or split the job into later invoices. The schedule carries from the quote to the job. A roof or a house that needs money before the day of the wash uses the deposit. The article says a payment schedule on a job, as opposed to a quote, is for a one-off job.

**Invoicing.** On Core, an invoice is created from the job and the customer can pay it online. Connect adds automatic reminders for unpaid invoices and for quotes that have not been approved.

**Payments.** Jobber Payments is on Core in the US, Canada, and the UK. The [pricing table](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) shows the same three rates on Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus: cards at 2.9% + 30¢, Tap to Pay on the phone at 2.7% + 30¢, and US bank payments (ACH) at 1%. The table shows one card rate. It does not publish a separate rate for American Express or for a card kept on file. The Core article says instant payouts to a debit card are available for an extra 1%. Customers can add a tip when they pay online.

[Automatic payments](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automatic-payments/) charge a card or ACH account saved through Jobber Payments when a recurring job's invoice is created. The Connect article names that feature. The automatic-payments article says the feature is on select plans and points at the pricing page. The charge follows the job's billing schedule: after each completed visit, on the last day of the month, on a custom schedule, or when the job is closed. "As needed" does not work with automatic payments. A failed charge is attempted once. The invoice stays open and someone collects it by hand. The pricing page says credit and debit card processing rates apply, and it does not publish a second rate for a card on file.

Consumer financing is [Wisetack](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-wisetack-consumer-financing-integration/), a flat 3.9% to the contractor, on every plan except Lite, United States only. The quote has to be a one-off residential job, in a person's name, titled, between $500 and $65,000, and the work has to be finished within 90 days. A repeating schedule is excluded, so the monthly house wash is out and a one-off roof can be in. The industries that article lists as ineligible are pool and spa, residential cleaning, commercial cleaning, window washing, and snow removal. Pressure washing is not named. Wisetack still has to approve the company. The article asks for a review history, 10 or more reviews averaging near 4.5 stars, and more than $12,500 a month or $150,000 a year. Funds arrive 1–3 business days after the customer confirms the work is done.

**Repeat visits.** This is the monthly or quarterly wash. [Recurring jobs](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/create-a-recurring-job/) are in the Core workflow. Visits repeat weekly, biweekly, monthly, or on a custom pattern. The job can end after a period or on a date. Jobber's own guidance is to end recurring jobs within a few years, because a very long schedule creates enough visits to slow the account down. Billing is per visit, or a fixed price for the period, such as one amount per month no matter how many visits fell in it. A single visit can carry different line items, and those lines transfer to a per-visit invoice. On a fixed-price job they do not transfer, because that invoice is not built from the visits.

[Job basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-basics/) says the job type cannot be switched later. A one-off spring wash cannot be turned into the monthly wash. Recreate it as a recurring job.

**QuickBooks.** Connect includes QuickBooks Online. The [current integration FAQ](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quickbooks-integration-faqs-new-quickbooks-integration/), updated October 29, 2026, says the new sync is rolling out to select accounts. It runs one way, Jobber to QuickBooks Online, on an ongoing basis. Clients, products and services, invoices, payments (including payouts, tips, and refunds), and timesheets sync. Paid status no longer comes back from QuickBooks into Jobber. The first connection can import clients and products from QuickBooks once. After that, edit those records in Jobber. Supported US versions named in that FAQ are Simple Start, Essentials, Plus, and Advanced. QuickBooks Desktop does not sync. A CSV of Desktop clients is a one-time file import.

**Mobile app.** The iOS and Android apps are how the Core user sees the day and takes payment. The FAQ on the pricing page says the mobile app is available in Spanish for team members, and not for administrators or the account owner.

## Housecall Pro

For pressure washing, the product on the [pricing page](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) is the standard plan. The [pressure washing page](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/pressure-washing-software/) describes quotes, one-time and recurring washes, invoices, and payment on site. It does not list plan prices, and it does not say Basic, Essentials, or MAX. Basic is the one-user card. Essentials adds routes, phone GPS, flat-rate pricing, and QuickBooks Online. MAX adds the visual proposal, recurring service plans, and live-traffic route optimization.

**Scheduling.** Basic includes scheduling, dispatch, and online booking, including booking from Google or the company website. That covers a one-person calendar. Essentials adds Routes, "group and sequence jobs to cut drive time for the day," plus employee GPS from the phone, with no hardware. MAX adds route optimization, "reorder jobs in one click based on real drive time and live traffic." A solo operator's day is the Basic calendar. The grouped route and the live-traffic reorder are the higher cards.

The pressure washing page says rain-delayed jobs can be moved, and that "On my way" texts go out. It does not name the plan for those messages.

**Quotes.** Basic sends estimates, and the price book is on Basic. MAX adds the sales proposal tool: visual proposals with images and side-by-side comparisons. The card lists that tool as a "$40/mo value" included on MAX. The pressure washing page describes multi-option estimates, photos, discounts, deposits, and a digital signature, and it does not say which of those are the MAX proposal and which are on Basic. Treat the side-by-side visual proposal as MAX. Treat a single estimate from the price book as Basic.

**Invoicing.** Basic includes estimates and invoicing, and the customer can pay from the invoice. The [QuickBooks sync article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6293215-quickbooks-online-syncing-information-from-housecall-pro) describes deposit invoices moving to QuickBooks. It does not say which plan can create the deposit. Estimates do not sync to QuickBooks Online.

**Payments.** Rates are from Housecall Pro's [payments article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2046930-housecall-pro-payment-processing-options), dated July 20, 2026, and the pricing FAQ.

- Card swiped, tapped, chipped, or taken with Tap to Pay: 2.59%.
- Tap to Pay also costs [$5 per active device](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/8279161-getting-started-with-tap-to-pay-on-mobile) in any month that device takes a Tap to Pay payment. The payments article says Tap to Pay works on an iPhone or an Android phone. A card reader is purchased separately in the app. That article does not list the reader's price.
- Card entered by the customer on an invoice: 2.99%.
- Card typed by staff, scanned in the app, or saved on file: 3.49%.
- American Express, and commercial, corporate, or business cards, any method: 3.49%.
- ACH: 1%. The [ACH FAQ](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2043865-ach-payments-faq) says the fee may vary by plan and says to confirm the rate in chat. The [Instapay FAQ](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/1801050-instapay-faq) says bank payments are available in the United States.
- PayPal and Venmo on an invoice: 2.99%. PayPal Pay Later and Klarna: 4.99%.
- Consumer financing: starting at 3.9%. The Basic card lists payments and consumer financing. The approval rules were not on the pages checked.
- Mobile check deposit: the pricing FAQ says depositing a check in the app has no fee. The payments article lists a 1–4 business day payout and no percentage for that method.
- Instant payout (Instapay) adds 1%.

[Automatic payments](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9944421-card-on-file-automatic-payments) charge a card on file either when the job is marked finished or on the invoice due date. The customer gets a receipt. The invoice itself is still sent by hand if you want them to see it. The article says the due-date trigger does not work for batch invoicing, and a charge that meets the rule is attempted for the full amount at that moment. It does not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX. The saved-card rate above is 3.49%.

**Repeat visits.** [Create a recurring job](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2789168-create-a-recurring-job) covers daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, and custom repeats, from the website or the mobile app. The same article says one visit or the whole series can be changed. It does not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX. Recurring jobs are not one of the lines the pricing card reserves for Essentials or MAX.

[Service plans](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2369524-create-service-plan-templates) are the other repeat-work tool, and the pricing card puts them on MAX. A template holds up to 12 included visits a year. Duration is until the customer cancels, or a set end. Billing is every month, every quarter, every six months, or every year. A wash that happens once a month uses 12 visits in a year, which is the cap. A wash every two weeks, or every week through a season, passes 12. If the plan is not payable by cash or check, the customer pays by card. If you accept cash or check and you do not collect the whole plan up front, Housecall Pro does not pull those installments. They show as due. A card kept for a service plan is a saved card, so the published rate is 3.49%.

**QuickBooks.** The onboarding guide, dated March 5, 2026, says you must be on Essentials or MAX to connect. It lists QuickBooks Online Simple Start, Essentials, Plus, and Advanced as the QuickBooks plans that connect. The Essentials card describes the feature as an import of existing QuickBooks data. After the connection, the [sync article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6293215-quickbooks-online-syncing-information-from-housecall-pro), dated September 3, 2026, says the integration typically runs one way, Housecall Pro to QuickBooks Online: invoices, payments (including service-plan payments), new customers, and new price-book items. An invoice edit in Housecall Pro can still need a manual push before QuickBooks updates. A script embedded on the pricing page lists "QuickBooks online and desktop" under Essentials. The card itself says QuickBooks Online. See unverified claims.

**Mobile app.** Every plan includes the iOS and Android app, in English and Spanish, and the pricing page lists offline viewing, phone and chat support, and the mobile app under "Included in every Housecall Pro plan." Recurring jobs can be created from the app.

## FreshBooks

FreshBooks is accounting and invoicing software. The [pricing page](https://www.freshbooks.com/pricing) sells it to a business that sends estimates and invoices and keeps the books in the same account. The pages checked for this guide describe time tracking and projects. They do not describe a calendar of washes, dispatch, or a customer booking a driveway online.

The plan that fits a solo washer is Plus, because of the client cap, not because Plus is the cheapest card. The pricing page and its FAQ set Lite at 5 billable clients, Plus at 50, and Premium and Select at unlimited. The [pricing help article](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006873232-What-is-the-pricing) says the limit counts active and archived clients. Deleting a client frees a spot. Deleted clients' invoices remain, and a deleted client cannot be invoiced until they are restored. Archiving does not free a spot.

**Scheduling.** [Time tracking](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/225525527-How-do-I-track-my-time) records hours against a client, a project, and a service, by duration or by a start and end time. The day, week, and month views are logs of that time. [Projects](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/227401948-What-are-projects) can hold a client, an end date, a budget of hours, and a flat rate. The iOS and Android time-tracking articles say projects are created in a browser. None of those pages is a schedule of where to be at 9 a.m.

**Quotes.** The comparison table marks unlimited estimates, and deposits, on Lite, Plus, Premium, and Select. Accepting an e-signature, and proposals, start on Plus. An estimate can be converted to an invoice in the [iOS](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008042227-How-do-I-create-estimates-on-iOS) and [Android](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/115010176427-How-do-I-create-estimates-on-Android) apps.

**Invoicing.** The comparison table marks unlimited invoices, recurring invoices, scheduled late fees, and automated late-payment reminders on Lite. The [iOS](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/227484828-How-do-I-create-invoices-on-iOS) and [Android](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/227483868-How-do-I-create-invoices-on-Android) articles walk through creating an invoice on the phone, including a deposit or a payment schedule, and emailing it. That is the on-site invoice: the customer receives a link and pays it.

**Payments.** Rates are from FreshBooks' [USD fee article](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/25362149006349-What-are-the-transaction-fees-for-FreshBooks-Payments-USD). The pricing-page FAQ states a shorter version, credit at 2.9% + $0.30 and ACH at 1%, and does not split card types.

- Standard credit and debit cards, when the customer pays: 2.9% + $0.30.
- Commercial, corporate, business, and American Express: 3.5% + $0.30.
- Bank transfer (ACH), including AutoPay by bank: 1%. The article says caps are available on Select plans and does not publish the cap.
- Managed payments, which the article defines as the virtual terminal, AutoPay by credit card, and AutoCollect: 3.5% + $0.30.
- Buy Now, Pay Later with Affirm, Afterpay, or Klarna: 6% + $0.30.
- Instant payouts: an additional 1.5%.
- A failed ACH: $4. A dispute: $15. An international card adds 1.5%. A currency that does not match the FreshBooks Payments account adds 2%.

[Advanced Payments](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/360007126671-What-are-Advanced-Payments) is the virtual terminal: key a card, store it, and charge an invoice, in person, over the phone, or from a computer. The [pricing cards](https://www.freshbooks.com/pricing) list it at $20 per month on Lite, Plus, and Premium. The help article says it is included on Select and is an extra cost on the other plans. Select does not publish a subscription price. The comparison table marks "Securely accept credit card payments in person or over the phone," "Charge client credit cards," and "Secure card storage" on Select. The [iOS article](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/360022510572-How-do-I-use-Advanced-Payments-on-iOS) says the phone can scan the card or the numbers can be typed. The [Android payments article](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008207608-How-do-I-accept-online-payments-on-Android) turns on online payments so the customer can pay. It does not document Charge Card. The Advanced Payments FAQ says a card the client entered on their side cannot be charged by the business. The business charges cards it keyed itself.

The practical split on a driveway: send the invoice and let the customer pay it, at 2.9% + $0.30 for a standard card, on Lite and up. Or add Advanced Payments, at $20 a month, and key or scan the card, at 3.5% + $0.30.

**Repeat customers.** A [recurring template](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/222843308-How-do-I-create-a-recurring-template) generates invoices weekly, monthly, yearly, or on a custom schedule. It can send them or save them as drafts. Requiring a credit card for those invoices is an Advanced Payments setting on the template. The template bills the customer. It does not put next month's wash on a route.

**QuickBooks.** The pricing FAQ asks whether FreshBooks can replace QuickBooks Online and answers by pointing at a trial of FreshBooks. [Importing](https://support.freshbooks.com/hc/en-us/articles/115007020007-How-do-I-import-data-into-my-account) describes a CSV import and Easy Switch, a one-time transfer from QuickBooks and other products, through a conversion partner. Easy Switch is included on some Select plans and offered at a discounted cost on Plus, Premium, and other Select plans. The pages checked do not describe a sync that keeps running after that import.

**Mobile app.** The pricing FAQ says the account is web-based, and that the iOS or Android app has to be installed to use it on a phone. The same answer says the phone can do basically all of the same business and accounting tasks as the desktop. Creating projects is the exception the time-tracking articles call out: do that in a browser.

## What to watch out for

A Jobber user, and a Housecall Pro user, is someone who opens the schedule. A helper who rides along and never logs in is not one of those users. A helper who needs the day's list on a phone is. Core and Housecall Pro Basic are both one user. FreshBooks bills a logging-in helper as a team member at $11 a month. The owner is the account, not that add-on.

FreshBooks also counts clients. Lite's 5 includes archived customers. A driveway you washed in March and might wash again is still a client until you delete the record. Plus, at 50, is the plan to budget. Premium is the plan if the list will pass 50 and those records have to stay.

Build the repeat wash in the shape the product stores. In Jobber, create a recurring job at the start. A one-off job will not convert later, and automatic payment of that series is named on Connect. In Housecall Pro, a repeating job is a recurring job, and the article never calls that tool MAX. A service plan stops at 12 visits a year, and buying MAX to "get recurring" buys the membership. In FreshBooks, a recurring template sends the invoice. The wash itself is still a date you keep.

Count the card rate you will actually pay. A card tapped on the washer's phone is Jobber's 2.7% + 30¢, or Housecall Pro's 2.59% plus $5 in a month that phone takes one of those payments. A card the customer pays on an invoice link is Jobber's 2.9% + 30¢, Housecall Pro's 2.99%, or FreshBooks' 2.9% + $0.30 for a standard consumer card. A saved card charged by the business is the rate to check before you turn on automatic billing: Jobber's pricing page points at the published card rate and does not list a second one, Housecall Pro publishes 3.49% for a saved card, and FreshBooks publishes 3.5% + $0.30 for card AutoPay and for the virtual terminal. American Express and business cards are called out at the higher rate by Housecall Pro and by FreshBooks. Jobber's pricing table shows one card rate.

Jobber's $29 and $99 prices are a one-year term. The no-commitment prices are $49 and $139. Housecall Pro's $59 Basic price is the annual figure on the card. The month-to-month figure next to it is $79, and the FAQ says there is no long-term contract. FreshBooks' $464.40 is the yearly Plus price, against $43 paid each month.

There is no pressure-washing package that moves Housecall Pro's MAX tools onto a cheaper card. Memberships and the visual proposal stay on MAX. QuickBooks stays on Essentials.

QuickBooks will drift if the same customers are edited in both products. Jobber's current Online sync is one way, out of Jobber, and Jobber says that version is still reaching select accounts. Jobber does not sync QuickBooks Desktop. Housecall Pro's Online article also describes a feed that typically runs one way, into QuickBooks, with a manual push when an invoice changes. FreshBooks' documented path out of QuickBooks is a one-time import.

Both field-service sites lead with results: hours saved, revenue growth. Those are the vendors' aggregates. Nothing on this page comes from our using any of these apps on a driveway.

## FAQ

**I wash alone. What is the least I can pay and still invoice on site?**

Jobber Core, if one login is enough: $29 per month billed annually, or $49 with no commitment. That includes scheduling, recurring jobs, quotes, invoices, and card payments, including the Tap to Pay rate on the pricing table. It does not include QuickBooks Online or automatic charging of repeat visits. Housecall Pro Basic is $59 per month billed annually, or $79 month-to-month, for one user, or $39.50 for the first three months against the $79. Basic includes a price book and job costing. FreshBooks Lite is $23 a month, or $248.40 a year, and it allows 5 clients.

**I have monthly customers. Which plan charges the card without a phone call after each wash?**

Jobber names that on Connect, for recurring jobs only, using a card or bank account saved through Jobber Payments. The pricing page points at the published card rates and does not list a separate saved-card rate. Housecall Pro documents an automatic charge of a card on file when a job is finished or when the invoice is due, at the saved-card rate of 3.49%. The article does not name the plan. A Housecall Pro service plan also charges a saved card, and that membership is on MAX, with at most 12 visits a year. FreshBooks can generate the invoice from a recurring template on Lite. Charging a card the business stored on that template is Advanced Payments, at $20 a month and 3.5% + $0.30.

**I already use QuickBooks. Which plan keeps syncing?**

Jobber Connect, for QuickBooks Online. One user is $99 billed annually or $139 month-to-month. The sync runs from Jobber to QuickBooks Online, and Jobber says the current version is still reaching select accounts. Jobber does not sync QuickBooks Desktop. Housecall Pro Essentials or MAX, which the onboarding guide requires before the connection. Essentials is $149 billed annually or $189 month-to-month and includes five users. The feed typically runs from Housecall Pro into QuickBooks Online. FreshBooks documents a one-time import from QuickBooks, not an ongoing sync. If the books are moving to FreshBooks, Plus is the plan with 50 clients.

**Can I take a card in the driveway?**

Jobber's published Tap to Pay rate is 2.7% + 30¢, on the pricing table for every plan including Core, with no separate hardware fee in that table. Housecall Pro's in-person and Tap to Pay rate is 2.59%, plus $5 in a month when that phone takes a Tap to Pay payment. A card typed into Housecall Pro, or saved on file, is 3.49%. FreshBooks' on-site path without an add-on is to email the invoice and let the customer pay it. Keying or scanning the card is Advanced Payments, documented as a scan or typed number on iOS, at $20 a month and 3.5% + $0.30.

**A helper needs the app. What changes?**

Jobber Core and Housecall Pro Basic are one user. Jobber Connect supports up to five. One Connect user is $99 billed annually, and the pricing card says another user is $29 a month. Housecall Pro's filled-in step up is Essentials, five users at $149 billed annually or $189 month-to-month. The price of a second user on Basic was `$XX` on the seat toggle. FreshBooks Plus stays the same subscription, and the helper is an $11 team member. That login can record time and, depending on the role, work in the account. It does not add a route.

**I want the customer to pick the house, the driveway, or both.**

Jobber Grow adds optional line items on the quote. One user is $149 billed annually or $199 month-to-month. Housecall Pro's side-by-side visual proposal is on MAX, eight users at $299 billed annually or $329 month-to-month. Estimates start on Basic. FreshBooks estimates start on Lite. Proposals, a separate document, and e-signatures start on Plus.
