A residential cleaning company is usually buying software for a week that is already booked: the Tuesday house, the every-other-week house, a first-clean or deep-clean quote, the invoice for that visit or for the month, and a card that should charge without a phone call after the team leaves. When a second cleaner or a small crew is on the schedule, the same week also needs the day's order, a reminder so the client expects the team, and a way for that client to see the next visits. This page compares [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) and [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) on that work for a maid service or house-cleaning company of one person up to about ten. It is a comparison of the vendors' pricing pages and help centers, checked on November 23, 2026. We have not run either product on a house.

A one-person cleaner who is also comparing Square, ZenMaid, BookingKoala, or FreshBooks should use the [solo house cleaning guide]({{ '/guides/best-software-solo-house-cleaning-business/' | relative_url }}). This page stays with Jobber and Housecall Pro, and with what changes when more than one person has to open the schedule.

## Who it's for

Use this if you run residential house cleaning or a maid service, you are the owner who still builds the week, and the company is one cleaner or a team of up to about ten. That includes a solo cleaner who invoices from the phone, an owner plus a few cleaners who each need the day's list, and a small crew that sends two people to the same house.

It is not a commercial janitorial system, and it is not Housecall Pro's HVAC, plumbing, or electrical package. The help center's [industry packages](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/collections/19689640-industry-packages) are those three trades. Cleaning is on Housecall Pro's standard plans. The pricing page fetched for this guide is headed for an "Other" business, and the pricing FAQ names cleaning alongside the trades. Home Cleaning is one of the industries in the plan quiz. It does not have its own price card.

## Quick answer

Prices below are the ones to budget. The pricing section has the date, the billing term, and the page each number came from.

**One login.** [Jobber Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan: **$29 per month billed annually**, or **$49 month-to-month**. Core includes the calendar, online booking, recurring jobs, quotes, invoices, client hub, and card payments. The Core article says on-my-way texts and optional line items are not on Core. Automated reminders, automatic charging of a saved card, routing, and QuickBooks Online are named on Connect. Google review requests are part of Marketing Suite, a **$99 per month** add-on on the pricing page, or included on Plus.

Housecall Pro Basic is the one-user card: **$59 per month billed annually**, or **$79 month-to-month**, with **$39.50 for the first three months** printed against the $79. Basic includes scheduling, online booking, estimates, invoices, a price book, job costing, review management, and payments. Routes, phone GPS, checklist automations, and QuickBooks Online are on the Essentials card. Live-traffic reordering, the visual proposal, and recurring service plans are on MAX.

Buy Core if one person needs the repeating houses on a calendar and a lower ongoing bill. Buy Basic if that person wants review requests and job costing on the entry plan. The solo guide is the page that puts those two prices next to the cleaning-specific products.

**About five people, each with the app.** Jobber Connect for five users is **$149 per month billed annually**, **$169 per month on a 1-year term billed monthly**, or **$199 month-to-month**. The Connect article names routing, automated reminders, automatic payments on recurring jobs, and QuickBooks Online. Jobber Grow for five users is **$229 billed annually**, **$259 on the 1-year monthly term**, or **$299 month-to-month**. Grow names optional line items, Find a Time, two-way texting, and job costing.

Housecall Pro Essentials is **$149 per month billed annually**, or **$189 month-to-month**, with five users included, or **$94.50 for three months** against the $189. That card lists Routes, phone GPS, checklist automations, flat-rate pricing, commissions, and QuickBooks Online. A sixth user on Essentials was `$XX` on the seat toggle. Live-traffic reordering is MAX, at **$299 billed annually** or **$329 month-to-month**, with eight users included and **$35 per month** for each user after that.

**About ten logins.** The Grow article says the plan supports up to 10 users. The 10-user Grow pack is **$299 billed annually**, **$349 on a 1-year monthly term**, or **$399 month-to-month**. The pricing page also lists a 10-user Connect pack at **$229 billed annually**. The Connect article, updated November 18, 2026, says Connect supports up to five users. See unverified claims. Named crews, as their own object on the schedule, are Plus. Plus for ten users is **$449 billed annually**, **$499 on a 1-year monthly term**, or **$599 month-to-month**.

Housecall Pro does not print a 10-user pack. From the MAX card, eight users are included and each user after that is $35 per month. Ten logins are $329 + $70 = **$399 month-to-month**, or $299 + $70 = **$369** on the annual per-month figure, if that $35 does not change on annual billing. Those are calculations from the printed rates, not a bundle the page advertises. The Essentials extra-user field was `$XX`, so this guide does not state a 10-user Essentials price.

## Choose Jobber if

Choose Jobber if the product you are buying is the repeating house, and you want that series on a plan you can name.

Core already stores a weekly, biweekly, or monthly clean, and the customer can approve a quote and pay in client hub. Connect, at the five-user annual price of $149, is the plan that names automatic charging of that series and a driving-distance reorder of anytime visits. Grow is the plan if one quote should offer the standard clean and the add-ons, the fridge and the inside of the oven, and the customer picks. A company that will assign each cleaner by name, and does not need a named crew on the calendar, can stay off Plus.

Choose Jobber if you will stay the year for the annual price, and if Google review requests can wait or can sit on the $99 Marketing Suite add-on. Plus is the plan that includes those review requests, and Plus starts at five users.

Jobber's Wisetack financing excludes residential and commercial cleaning. A financing offer on a deep-clean quote is not a reason to pick Jobber for this trade.

## Choose Housecall Pro if

Choose Housecall Pro if review requests have to be on the plan you start with. Basic lists review management. Jobber's review requests are the Marketing Suite add-on, or Plus.

Choose Housecall Pro if the week is organized as routes: the same pair of cleaners, or the north-side houses, as a column on the calendar. The Essentials card is the one that lists Routes, and the routes article is the help-center description of a named route with the employees on it. That article does not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX. Confirm it on an Essentials trial if a route column is the requirement. Phone GPS, with no hardware, is on the Essentials card.

Choose Housecall Pro if the day's houses already have clock times and you want them reordered from live traffic. That tool is MAX, and a visit with a fixed arrival window can be locked so the reorder leaves it alone. Jobber's route reorder applies to anytime visits, the ones with no start and end time.

Choose Housecall Pro if you do not want a one-year subscription term. The pricing FAQ says the plans do not require a long-term contract. Jobber's annual price and its 1-year monthly price are a 12-month term.

A weekly clean belongs on a recurring job. Recurring service plans are on MAX, and a service-plan template holds up to 12 included visits a year. A weekly house is about 52 visits. An every-other-week house is about 26. A monthly house uses 12, which is the cap. The tool that matches a residential route is a recurring job. The recurring-job article does not name a plan.

## Jobber

Jobber's [cleaning page](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/cleaning-business-software/) describes one-time and recurring cleans, quotes, a client hub, reminders, and review requests in one account. It does not name Core, Connect, Grow, or Plus. The plan articles and the pricing table do.

**Scheduling, routing, and crews.** Core includes requests, online booking, the calendar, and jobs. A job is one-off or recurring. [Job basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-basics/), updated November 12, 2026, uses weekly cleaning as the recurring example and a move-out clean as the one-off example. A one-off job can hold up to 20 visits and still is not a repeating schedule. The same article says the job type cannot be switched later. A one-off deep clean cannot be turned into the weekly clean. Recreate it.

[Route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/route-optimization-new-schedule/), updated November 13, 2026, is on Connect, Grow, and Plus. An admin reorders the day on Jobber.com. The app then shows the new order. Only anytime visits, the ones with no start and end time, are reordered. A clean you put on the calendar at 10:00 a.m. stays put. Optimization uses driving distance, can cover one person or the whole team, and can run up to seven days at a time. The first and last stop can be pinned. Only an admin can run it.

[Find a Time](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/new-schedule/), on Grow and Plus, is the tool that places a timed visit. Open slots account for the length of the appointment and drive time between the previous and next jobs. The schedule does not add drive time from home or the shop on the first or last appointment of the day. Suggested slots and assignees with the shortest drive show in green. The schedule itself is on every plan. Find a Time is not.

The Connect article says the plan can route the day's work and assign an incoming job to the closest person on the map. That sentence is broader than the anytime-visit optimizer. This guide does not treat them as the same button. Connect also lists live vehicle GPS through FleetSharp. The article does not say the hardware is included in the subscription.

[Crew scheduling](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/crew-scheduling/), updated November 6, 2026, is Plus, and only on Jobber.com. It is not in the app. A crew has a name and its members. Assigning the crew puts every member on the visit in one step. A person can belong to only one crew at a time. A crew lead is not supported yet. Moving someone to another crew updates that person's upcoming visits, including every upcoming visit of a recurring job. On Core, Connect, and Grow, the recurring-job form assigns team members one assignment at a time. Plus starts at five users on the pricing page. A two-person team that can be assigned by name does not need Plus to put both cleaners on the visit.

The Connect article also names custom fields, and its examples are an entry code, pets at the property, and a referral name. Those fields can be a column on a report. The article says Connect supports up to five users. A user, on the pricing page, is anyone who opens the account, in the office or in the field, to view or manage the schedule.

**Online booking and client hub.** Core includes request forms and online booking. A form can sit on a website, a social page, or in client hub. The cleaning page says the included website works with your own domain and does not name the plan. The pricing page lists a professional website among Core's key features. [Client hub settings](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/client-hub-settings/), updated November 17, 2026, are on all plans. The customer approves a quote, looks at appointments, pays an invoice, prints a receipt, or requests more work, from a link, without a password.

[What the customer sees](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/what-do-your-clients-see-in-client-hub/), updated the same day, is specific about a repeating schedule. The appointment list shows the next five upcoming visits, as long as they fall within five years, and the past five, with a button for more past visits. Opening a visit shows the date, the time or the arrival window, the address, and the first name and photo of anyone assigned. The clock time can be hidden in settings. If it has been more than 14 days since a client-hub link was sent, the customer may be asked for an email address or the last four digits of a phone number. Anyone who receives the link can open that client's hub. The link cannot be reset.

On Grow, the customer can select optional line items in client hub, and the total updates. The Grow article also names a refer-a-friend prompt in client hub. That prompt is separate from the referrals tool inside Marketing Suite.

**Quotes.** Core quotes use your branding. There is no cap on how many you send, and the customer approves them in client hub. Optional line items are not on Core. Grow adds them: add-ons, or packages the customer picks between, plus images and a markup that shows an estimated margin. The cleaning page describes that choice, a deep clean or an add-on such as interior windows or laundry, and does not name Grow. The pricing page says reviews inside that advanced quote layout require the Reviews add-on.

**Invoicing.** On Core, an invoice is created from the job and the customer can pay it online. A recurring job bills per visit, or a fixed price for the period, such as one amount per month. Visit-specific line items, the one-time oven on a regular clean, transfer to a per-visit invoice. On a fixed-price job they do not transfer. Connect adds automatic reminders for unpaid invoices and for quotes that have not been approved.

**Payments.** Jobber Payments is on Core in the US, Canada, and the UK. The [pricing table](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) shows the same three rates on Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus: cards at 2.9% + 30¢, Tap to Pay on the phone at 2.7% + 30¢, and US bank payments (ACH) at 1%. The table shows one card rate. It does not publish a separate rate for American Express or for a card kept on file. The Core article says instant payouts to a debit card are available for an extra 1%. In client hub, the customer can add a tip when paying an invoice.

[Automatic payments](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automatic-payments/), updated October 21, 2026, charge a card or ACH account saved through Jobber Payments when a recurring job's invoice is created. The Connect article names that feature. The automatic-payments article says select plans. The charge follows the job's billing schedule: after each completed visit, on the last day of the month, on a custom schedule, or when the job is closed. "As needed" does not work with automatic payments. A failed charge is attempted once. The invoice stays open. A monthly fixed price is created and charged at 9 p.m. in the company's timezone on the scheduled day, so a clean done that day is still included. The customer gets a receipt email.

Consumer financing is [Wisetack](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-wisetack-consumer-financing-integration/), a flat 3.9% to the contractor, on every plan except Lite, United States only. The article, updated October 29, 2026, lists the industries that are not eligible: pool and spa, residential cleaning, commercial cleaning, window washing, and snow removal. A house cleaning business is on that list. The repeating-schedule rule would have excluded the weekly clean anyway. The industry rule excludes the one-off deep clean as well.

**Repeat visits.** [Recurring jobs](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/create-a-recurring-job/), updated November 12, 2026, are in the Core workflow. Visits repeat weekly, biweekly, monthly, or on a custom pattern, including two days a week or the first and third Friday. Jobber's own guidance is to end recurring jobs within a few years, because a very long schedule creates enough visits to slow the account down. A job close to its end can be extended by editing the end date. There is no membership the customer accepts on the quote with a separate visit cap.

**Reminders and reviews.** The Connect plan card on the pricing page adds "Send automated client reminders" on top of Core. The Connect article describes automated emails and texts, including appointment reminders. The Core article says on-my-way texts are not on Core. Job basics says a booking-confirmation email is on select plans and does not name the plan.

[Reviews](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/reviews-marketing-tools/), updated November 18, 2026, send a Google review request by email, text, or both, after a job closes, a visit is completed, or an invoice is paid in full. The same article says Jobber sends one request to a given client every 6 months, even if the trigger is every completed visit on a recurring job. Follow-ups, if you leave them on, go out at 1:30 p.m. local time. A request triggered after 9 p.m. or before 9 a.m. goes by email only. Reviews are available in the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom on all plans as part of Marketing Suite. The Core and Grow articles say Marketing Suite is an add-on, except on Lite, where it is not offered. The pricing page lists Marketing Suite at $99 per month. The Plus article says Plus includes it. Add-ons are not available during the trial. Replies can be drafted from the reviews dashboard on Jobber.com. The article says a suggested reply is generated for you to edit before you post it.

**Job costing.** The [job costing article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-costing/), updated November 12, 2026, says costing is on Grow and Plus and covers one-off and recurring jobs. On a recurring job the math uses the last 30 days, not the life of the house, and job-cost reporting is not available for recurring jobs. The Grow plan article, updated November 18, 2026, describes job costing on one-off jobs. Those two pages disagree. See unverified claims. Housecall Pro lists job costing on Basic, which is the comparison if a per-clean margin on the entry plan is the reason to switch.

**QuickBooks.** The Connect article names QuickBooks Online. The pricing page's feature table titles the line "QuickBooks Online and Xero sync." The pricing FAQ says Jobber syncs with QuickBooks Online and does not describe Xero in that answer.

**Mobile app.** The iOS and Android apps are how each paid user sees the day and takes payment. The pricing FAQ says Spanish is available to team members other than administrators and the account owner. Route optimization and crew scheduling stay on the website. Recurring jobs can be created in the app.

## Housecall Pro

For a cleaning business, the product on the [pricing page](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) is the standard plan. The [maid service page](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/maid-service-software/) describes recurring cleans, online booking, invoices, a customer portal, and payment. It does not list plan prices, and it does not say Basic, Essentials, or MAX. There is no cleaning package that moves MAX tools onto Essentials.

**Scheduling, routing, and crews.** Basic includes scheduling, dispatch, and online booking. That covers a one-person calendar and assigning the job to a cleaner. The Essentials card adds Routes, described there as grouping and sequencing jobs to cut drive time for the day, plus employee GPS from the phone, with no hardware.

The [routes article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/13871869-getting-started-with-routes-in-housecall-pro) is the longer description, and it does not name a plan. A route has a name, a color, and one or more employees. Examples in the article are a territory, a morning crew, and a downtown route. The calendar can show columns by route instead of by person. Assigning the route puts those employees on the job. One person is either on the job through the route or assigned individually, not both. A sick day is a change to that day: add or remove someone on the day's column, and the route template stays as it was. The route view shows up to 15 routes. The employee view shows up to 15 employees. Reporting filtered by route is described as coming soon. See unverified claims for whether this article is the Essentials line on the card.

[Optimize by Drive Time](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/14093813-optimize-by-drive-time), dated August 24, 2026, is MAX. It reorders one cleaner's jobs for one day, using road distance and live traffic. It does not move the job to another day or another cleaner. The day starts and ends, by default, at the company address in organization settings, and that address can be changed, including to a home. A job with an arrival window that cannot move can be locked. Jobs marked anytime can be placed anywhere in the day that shortens the drive, inside business hours. Saving can notify the customers whose times changed, or save without notifying them.

The maid page says a crew can be dispatched by location, that "On my way" texts go out, and that reminders go out before the visit. It does not name the plan.

**Online booking and the customer portal.** Basic includes online booking from Google or the company website. The maid page says the booking page can list cleaning services, prices, and service areas, and that Reserve with Google can book from Search and Maps. It does not name the plan. The routes article says that once routes are on, online booking checks route availability and assigns the booking to a route that has the slot.

The [customer portal](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/8448657-using-the-customer-portal), dated July 16, 2026, lets the customer see upcoming and past appointments, pay invoices, and view estimates. An invite email logs them in without a password. The link in that email stays active for 7 days, and an expired link triggers a new one. If online booking is set up, the portal has a Book Online button, and the customer needs a first and last name on the profile to see it. A message button, for a new appointment, a reschedule, or a cancellation, shows only if the company has a custom SMS number and has registered it. The article does not name a plan. If Housecall Pro Payments is in use, the customer can add, update, or remove a card on file from the portal.

**Quotes and invoicing.** Basic sends estimates, and the price book is on Basic. MAX adds the sales proposal tool: visual proposals with images and side-by-side comparisons. The card lists that tool as a "$40/mo value" included on MAX. A single estimate for a first clean is Basic. Two designed options on one proposal are MAX. Basic includes invoicing, and the customer can pay from the invoice. Essentials adds checklist automations. The recurring-job form can attach checklists. The plan gate this guide uses for checklist automations is the Essentials card.

**Payments.** Rates are from Housecall Pro's [payments article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2046930-housecall-pro-payment-processing-options), dated July 20, 2026, and the pricing FAQ.

- Card swiped, tapped, chipped, or taken with Tap to Pay: 2.59%.
- Tap to Pay also costs $5 per active device in any month that device takes a Tap to Pay payment.
- Card entered by the customer on an invoice: 2.99%.
- Card typed by staff, scanned in the app, or saved on file: 3.49%.
- American Express, and commercial, corporate, or business cards, any method: 3.49%.
- ACH: 1%.
- PayPal and Venmo on an invoice: 2.99%. PayPal Pay Later and Klarna: 4.99%.
- Consumer financing: starting at 3.9%. The Basic card lists payments and consumer financing. The payments article says you apply under My Money, and that you are paid in full once the job is confirmed complete. The approval rules, and whether a cleaning business is eligible, were not on the pages checked.
- Mobile check deposit: the pricing FAQ says depositing a check in the app has no fee. The payments article lists a 1–4 business day payout and no percentage for that method.
- Instant payout (Instapay) adds 1%.

[Automatic payments](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9944421-card-on-file-automatic-payments), dated July 23, 2026, charge a card on file either when the job is marked finished or on the invoice due date. The customer gets a receipt. The invoice itself is still sent by hand if you want them to see it. The due-date trigger does not work for batch invoicing. There is a 5- to 10-minute delay after the trigger. The article does not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX. The saved-card rate above is 3.49%.

## What to watch out for

A user is someone who opens the schedule. Jobber's pricing page says that directly. A helper who rides along and never logs in is not one of those users. A cleaner who needs the day's list on a phone is. Core and Basic are both one user on the plan articles and the cards. Five people with five phones is a five-user plan. Ten Housecall Pro logins are not the five-user Essentials price, and the price of users six through ten on Essentials was not on the page.

Build the repeat clean in the shape the product stores. In Jobber, create a recurring job at the start. A one-off deep clean will not convert later, and automatic payment of that series is named on Connect. End the series within a few years, then extend it, because Jobber says a very long schedule slows the account down. In Housecall Pro, a repeating house is a recurring job, and the article never calls that tool MAX. The series is generated a year out. A service plan stops at 12 visits a year, and buying MAX to "get recurring" buys the membership. A weekly client will not fit that field.

Count the card rate you will actually pay on a route that charges itself. A card the customer pays on an invoice link is Jobber's 2.9% + 30¢, or Housecall Pro's 2.99%. A saved card charged by the business is the rate to check before you turn on automatic billing. Housecall Pro publishes 3.49% for a card on file. Jobber's pricing page points at the published card rate and does not list a second one. Tap to Pay on Housecall Pro adds $5 for each phone that takes one of those payments in a month. Jobber's Tap to Pay rate is 2.7% + 30¢, with no per-device fee on the pricing table. American Express and business cards are called out at 3.49% by Housecall Pro. Jobber's pricing table shows one card rate.

Jobber's route reorder is an admin action on the website, for anytime visits. It does not move a visit that already has a clock time. Find a Time, on Grow and Plus, is the drive-time tool for a timed clean, and it skips the drive from home on the first and last stop of the day. Housecall Pro's live-traffic reorder is MAX, it runs one cleaner at a time, and a visit with a fixed window can be locked. The Essentials card's Routes line is the grouped day. The help article that describes a named crew-or-territory route does not name Essentials.

Client hub shows the next five upcoming visits. A weekly client who wants the next three months on one screen is past that list. The past list starts at five and can show more. Housecall Pro's portal article describes upcoming and past tabs and does not state a count.

Reviews are priced differently. Housecall Pro lists review management on Basic. Jobber's Google review requests require Marketing Suite at $99 per month, unless the plan is Plus, which starts at five users and $399 billed annually. Jobber also caps those requests at one per client every six months. Housecall Pro's default is to ask when the job is finished, and a recurring series creates a new job each visit.

Jobber's $29 and $149 prices, for one Core user and for five Connect users, are a one-year term when they are the annual figures. The no-commitment prices are $49 and $199. Housecall Pro's $59 Basic price and $149 Essentials price are the annual figures on the cards. The month-to-month figures next to them are $79 and $189, and the FAQ says there is no long-term contract. The 50% offer is three months. Budget the regular price.

There is no cleaning package that moves Housecall Pro's MAX tools onto a cheaper card. Service plans and the visual proposal stay on MAX. QuickBooks stays on Essentials. Jobber's Wisetack article excludes residential and commercial cleaning, so a financing offer on the quote is not a reason to pick Jobber for this trade.

Both sites lead with results: pros on the platform, hours saved, revenue growth. Those are the vendors' aggregates. Nothing on this page comes from our using either app on a house.

## FAQ

**I clean houses every week and every other week. Which plan puts them on a calendar?**

Jobber Core, as a recurring job. Weekly, biweekly, monthly, and custom patterns are in the recurring-job article, and the Core workflow includes recurring jobs. One user is $29 per month billed annually, or $49 with no commitment. Automatic charging of that series is named on Connect. Housecall Pro's recurring-job article covers a repeating visit and does not name Basic. The maid page lists weekly and biweekly visits and does not name a plan. A Housecall Pro service plan is a different tool, on MAX, and it stops at 12 visits a year.

**Five of us need the app. What bill should we expect?**

Use a five-user plan. Jobber Connect is $199 month-to-month or $149 billed annually, with a one-year term on the annual price. That plan's article names routing, reminders, and automatic payments. Jobber Grow is $299 month-to-month or $229 billed annually if the quote needs optional add-ons or you want Find a Time. Housecall Pro Essentials is $189 month-to-month or $149 billed annually, with five users included, or $94.50 for the first three months against the $189. A sixth user on Essentials was `$XX`. Live-traffic reordering is MAX.

**Can the client see the next cleans and pay a card without calling us?**

In Jobber, client hub is on every plan. The client can pay an invoice there, and the appointment list shows the next five upcoming visits. Charging a saved card when the recurring invoice is created is named on Connect. In Housecall Pro, the customer portal shows upcoming and past appointments and can take payment on an invoice. The portal article does not name a plan. An automatic charge of a card on file is documented, at the saved-card rate of 3.49%, and that article does not name the plan. The customer gets a receipt. The invoice is still sent by hand if you want them to see it.

**Which plan asks for a Google review after a clean?**

Housecall Pro Basic lists review management. The default send is when the job is marked finished, and the timing can be changed. Replies that post back to Google are Premium Reviews. The March 5, 2026 article says that feature is on Essentials and higher, and the English pricing card does not list it. Jobber's review requests are Marketing Suite, at $99 per month, or included on Plus. Jobber sends one request per client every six months, including when the clean repeats.

**We send two cleaners to the same house. Which plan treats them as a crew?**

Jobber Plus. Crew scheduling groups people into a named crew and assigns that crew in one step. It is on the website, not in the app, and a person can be on one crew at a time. Plus for five users is $499 month-to-month or $399 billed annually. On Connect or Grow, assign the two cleaners on the visit. Housecall Pro's routes article says a route can hold more than one employee, and assigning the route assigns those employees. The article does not name a plan. The Essentials card is the card that lists Routes. A one-day change, when one cleaner calls out, is described in that article and does not rewrite the route template.

**The customer wants to book the clean online. What is on the entry plan?**

Jobber Core includes online booking and request forms. The Connect article adds booking from Google through Local Services Ads. Housecall Pro Basic includes online booking from Google or the company website. The maid page describes Reserve with Google and a booking page with your prices, and it does not name the plan. Neither entry plan includes QuickBooks Online. That sync is Jobber Connect, or Housecall Pro Essentials.
