A small lawn or landscape company is usually buying software for a Monday route: the same yards every week, a rain day that moves half of them, a monthly bill that should go out without retyping each stop, and the occasional cleanup or patio quote that is not a mow. This page compares [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) and [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) on that work. It is a comparison of the vendors' pricing pages and help centers, checked on November 19, 2026. We have not run either product on a route.

## Who it's for

Use this if you mow, maintain, or landscape with one person or a crew of up to about ten, and the owner still builds the week. That includes a solo mower who invoices from the truck, a crew that runs recurring residential routes, and a small landscape company that also sells cleanups, plantings, or design-build work.

It is not a comparison with lawn-only products such as Service Autopilot, Yardbook, or Lawn Pro. It is also not Housecall Pro's HVAC package. Lawn and landscaping are on Housecall Pro's standard plans. The help center publishes separate package overviews for HVAC, plumbing, and electrical, and none for lawn.

## Quick answer

Prices below are the ones to budget. The pricing section has the date, the billing term, and the page each number came from.

**Solo mower, one login.** [Jobber Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan: **$29 per month billed annually**, or **$49 month-to-month**. Core includes the calendar, recurring jobs, quotes, invoices, online booking, and the client portal. Route optimization is documented on Connect, Grow, and Plus. Automatic charging of a saved card is named on Connect.

Housecall Pro Basic is the one-user card: **$59 per month billed annually**, or **$79 month-to-month**, with a three-month promotion at $39.50 printed against the $79. Basic includes scheduling, estimates, invoices, online booking, a price book, and job costing. The recurring-job help article does not name a plan. The live-traffic route tool is on MAX.

Buy Core if one person needs the route on a calendar and a lower bill. Buy Basic if that same person wants job costing without moving to Jobber Grow, which is **$149 per month billed annually** for one user.

**Small crew, recurring mowing routes.** For about five people who each open the app, put these two five-user prices next to each other. Jobber Connect is **$149 per month billed annually**, or **$199 month-to-month**. The Connect article lists routing, and automatic payments on recurring jobs are named there. Housecall Pro Essentials is **$149 per month billed annually**, or **$189 month-to-month**, with five users included. Essentials adds "Routes," which the card describes as grouping and sequencing jobs to cut drive time. The one-click reorder that uses real drive time and live traffic is on MAX, at **$299 per month billed annually** or **$329 month-to-month**, with eight users included.

Connect is the plan that matches a weekly mowing route if optimized stop order and automatic payment of those visits are the requirement. Essentials is the plan that matches if phone GPS, flat-rate prices, and job costing matter at that same annual subscription, and a grouped route is enough.

A sixth through tenth login is where the price lists stop lining up. Jobber sells a 10-user Connect pack at **$229 billed annually**. The Connect help article, updated November 18, 2026, still says the plan supports up to five users. Housecall Pro's extra-user price on Essentials was a `$XX` placeholder. See unverified claims.

**Landscaping and design-build, with larger quotes.** Jobber Grow is where a quote can offer add-ons or packages, with images and a markup. Five users are **$229 per month billed annually**, or **$299 month-to-month**. One user is **$149 billed annually**. Payment schedules, which split a one-off job into a deposit and later invoices, are documented on every Jobber plan. Wisetack financing is 3.9% to the contractor on one-off residential quotes, on every plan except Lite, if Wisetack approves the company. Repeating mowing is excluded from that financing.

Housecall Pro's visual proposal, side-by-side options with images, is on MAX. The card calls it a "$40/mo value" included in the plan. Estimates themselves start on Basic. A design-build shop that needs the proposal is comparing MAX's published price, eight users at $299 billed annually, with Grow. A shop that needs a staged invoice and a quote with choices, and can live inside a quote, is on Grow at the five-user price above. The single-user MAX price on the seat toggle was `$XX`.

## Jobber

Jobber's lawn page describes quotes, recurring visits, routes, invoicing, and a client portal in one account. The plan articles say which of those a crew of this size actually gets. [Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan. [Connect](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-connect-plan/) is where routing, QuickBooks Online, and automatic payments are named. [Grow](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-grow-plan/) is where a quote can carry add-ons and where job costing is listed. [Plus](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-plus-plan/) is the crew-scheduling and marketing tier.

**Scheduling and dispatch.** Core includes requests, online booking, the calendar, and jobs. A job is one-off or recurring. Online booking can land on the calendar from a form. [Client hub](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/client-hub-settings/) is on every plan: the customer approves quotes, looks at appointments, pays invoices, and requests more work from a link, without a password. The appointment list shows the next five upcoming visits, as long as they fall within five years, and the past five.

[Route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/route-optimization-new-schedule/) is on Connect, Grow, and Plus. That article, updated November 13, 2026, is specific about how a mowing route behaves. An admin reorders the day on Jobber.com, not in the phone app. The app then shows the new order. Only anytime visits, the ones with no start and end time, are reordered. Visits with a clock time stay put. Optimization uses driving distance between stops. It can cover one person, a group, or the whole team, up to seven days at a time, and it can leave the first and last stop where you pinned them. The Core article, updated November 18, 2026, does not list routing.

The same route article also documents selecting many appointments on the schedule or the map and moving or reassigning them together. That is the documented way to slide a rain day. A [visits article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/visits/) says the reschedule notice on a recurring job covers the visit you changed, and that this notice is for a visit moved on its own, not a bulk reassignment.

[Crew scheduling](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/crew-scheduling/) is Plus only, and only on Jobber.com. It groups people into a named crew and assigns that crew in one step. On Core, Connect, and Grow, the recurring-job form assigns team members to the visits one assignment at a time. Plus is the plan if the office needs the crew as its own object. It is a large step up in price for a mowing route that can already be assigned by person.

Connect also lists live vehicle GPS through FleetSharp. [The device article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/fleetsharp-tracker-devices/) says the trackers are purchased on a FleetSharp contract, so the hardware is outside the Jobber subscription. The article does not publish a single device price.

**Quotes.** Core quotes use your branding, there is no cap on how many you send, and the customer approves them in client hub. Optional line items are not on Core. Grow adds them: add-ons, or packages the customer picks between, plus images and a markup that shows an estimated margin. That is the lawn version of "mow, or mow plus aeration and a fall cleanup" on one quote. Line items come from the products and services list. Connect follows up on a quiet quote on a preset schedule. Grow can change the timing of those follow-ups in the custom automation builder.

**Invoicing.** On Core, an invoice is created from the job and the customer can pay it online. Connect adds automatic reminders for unpaid invoices and unapproved quotes. [Batch create](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/batch-create-invoices/) builds one draft invoice per client from the jobs that are ready to bill, and combines a client's ready jobs onto that invoice unless the properties use different tax rates. The article does not name a plan. [Payment schedules](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/progress-invoicing/) are on every plan. They split a one-off job into a deposit and later invoices, by percent or by dollar amount. The article says to use them on a one-off job, not a recurring one. A patio or planting that bills at start and at the end fits. A weekly mow does not use this tool.

**Payments.** Jobber Payments is on Core in the US, Canada, and the UK. The [pricing table](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) shows the same three rates on Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus: cards at 2.9% + 30¢, Tap to Pay on the phone at 2.7% + 30¢, and US bank payments (ACH) at 1%. The Core article says instant payouts to a debit card are available for an extra 1%. Customers can add a tip when they pay online.

[Automatic payments](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automatic-payments/) charge a card or ACH account saved through Jobber Payments when a recurring job's invoice is created. The Connect article names that feature. The pricing page says credit and debit card processing rates apply, and it does not publish a second rate for a card on file. The charge follows the job's billing schedule: after each completed visit, on the last day of the month, on a custom schedule, or when the job is closed. "As needed" does not work with automatic payments. A failed charge is attempted once. The invoice stays open and someone collects it by hand.

Consumer financing is [Wisetack](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-wisetack-consumer-financing-integration/), a flat 3.9% to the contractor, on every plan except Lite, United States only. The quote has to be a one-off residential job, in a person's name, titled, between $500 and $65,000, and the work has to be finished within 90 days. A repeating schedule is excluded, so the weekly mow is out and a one-off install can be in. The industries that article lists as ineligible are pool and spa, residential and commercial cleaning, window washing, and snow removal. Lawn care is not on that list. Wisetack still has to approve the company. The article asks for a review history, 10 or more reviews averaging near 4.5 stars, and more than $12,500 a month or $150,000 a year. Funds arrive 1–3 business days after the customer confirms the work is done.

**Recurring visits.** This is the mowing contract. [Recurring jobs](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/create-a-recurring-job/) are in the Core workflow. Visits repeat weekly, biweekly, monthly, or on a custom pattern, including two days a week or the first and third Friday. The job can end after a period or on a date. Jobber's own guidance is to end recurring jobs within a few years, because a very long schedule creates enough visits to slow the account down. Billing is per visit, or a fixed price for the period, such as one amount per month no matter how many visits fell in it. A single visit can carry different line items, for a one-time aeration on a mow, and those lines transfer to a per-visit invoice. On a fixed-price job they do not transfer, because that invoice is not built from the visits.

[Job basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-basics/) says the job type cannot be switched later. A one-off spring cleanup cannot be turned into the weekly mow. Recreate it as a recurring job.

[Chemical tracking](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/chemical-tracking/) is the treatment record: company and applicator license numbers, the chemical and concentration, tank mixes, the property, the job, the date, application details, and the weather. The list can be sorted and downloaded as a CSV. The article says this is for jurisdictions that require chemical or pesticide records for up to seven years, and that the form is filled in on the website. There is no chemical tracking in the mobile app. The article says the feature is on select plans and does not name them. The job-basics article shows it on an example Grow job and still says select plans.

**QuickBooks.** Connect includes QuickBooks Online. The [current integration FAQ](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quickbooks-integration-faqs-new-quickbooks-integration/), updated October 29, 2026, says the new sync is rolling out to select accounts. It runs one way, Jobber to QuickBooks Online, on an ongoing basis. Clients, products and services, invoices, payments (including payouts, tips, and refunds), and timesheets sync. Paid status no longer comes back from QuickBooks into Jobber. The first connection can import clients and products from QuickBooks once. After that, edit those records in Jobber. Supported US versions named in that FAQ are Simple Start, Essentials, Plus, and Advanced. [QuickBooks Desktop does not sync.](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quickbooks-integration-faqs-new-quickbooks-integration/) A CSV of Desktop clients is a one-time file import.

**Job costing, for the install and for the route.** The [job costing article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-costing/), updated November 12, 2026, says costing is on Grow and Plus and covers one-off and recurring jobs. Profit uses labor from timesheets, line-item unit costs, and expenses logged on the job. On a recurring job the article calculates from the last 30 days, not the life of the contract, and it says job-cost reporting is not available for recurring jobs. The Grow plan article, updated November 18, 2026, describes job costing on one-off jobs. Those two pages disagree. See unverified claims. Profit alerts, which flag a one-off job under a margin you set, are Plus, and the costing article says they do not include recurring jobs.

**Mobile app.** The iOS and Android apps are how the Core user, or each paid user on a higher plan, sees the day and takes payment. Jobber's pricing FAQ says the mobile app is available in Spanish for team members, and not for administrators or the account owner. Route optimization and chemical tracking stay on the website.

## Housecall Pro

For lawn and landscaping, the product on the [pricing page](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) is the standard plan, shown there as "your Other business" when no industry package is selected. Basic is the starting card. Essentials adds routes, phone GPS, flat-rate pricing, and QuickBooks Online. MAX adds the visual proposal, recurring service plans, and live-traffic route optimization. There is no lawn package overview that moves those MAX items onto Essentials the way the HVAC package does for memberships.

**Scheduling and dispatch.** Basic includes scheduling, dispatch, and online booking, including booking from Google or the company website. That covers a one-person calendar and assigning the job to a tech. Essentials adds Routes, "group and sequence jobs to cut drive time for the day," plus employee GPS from the phone, with no hardware. MAX adds route optimization, "reorder jobs in one click based on real drive time and live traffic." Those are two different lines on the card. A grouped list of the day's lawns is Essentials. A reorder that the card ties to live traffic is MAX.

The [lawn page](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/lawn-care-software/) says crews can drag visits when rain, a crew change, or a customer request moves the day, and that "On my way" texts go out with GPS. It does not name the plan for those messages. The Essentials card is the plan that names phone GPS.

[Service-plan scheduling suggestions](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/8124459-service-plan-scheduling-suggestions) are a separate tool, for placing a service-plan visit near other jobs. That article says the distance is straight-line, not drive time.

**Recurring visits.** [Create a recurring job](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2789168-create-a-recurring-job) covers daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, and custom repeats, from the website or the mobile app. [Each visit is its own job](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/12892543-appointments-vs-segments-vs-recurring-jobs), with its own schedule, invoice, and job number. Daily, weekly, and monthly series are extended one year out, and yearly series five years, and then extended again. You can change one visit or this visit and all future ones. An existing job can be turned into the first visit of a series. A recurring job cannot also be a multi-appointment job or a segmented job. The create article does not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX. Recurring jobs are not one of the lines the pricing card reserves for Essentials or MAX.

[Service plans](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2369524-create-service-plan-templates) are the other repeat-work tool, and the pricing card puts them on MAX. A template holds up to 12 included visits a year. Duration is until the customer cancels, or a set end. Billing is every month, every quarter, every six months, or every year. A weekly route passes 12 visits in a normal mowing season. A biweekly route does too, once the season runs past half a year. A fertilization or aeration program of a few visits a season can fit. If the plan is not payable by cash or check, the customer pays by card, and the [FAQ](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/5485025-service-plans-faqs) says a plan that continues until canceled charges the card on file at that frequency. If you accept cash or check and you do not collect the whole plan up front, Housecall Pro does not pull those installments. They show as due. A card kept for a service plan is a saved card, so the published rate is 3.49%.

**Quotes.** Basic sends estimates. MAX adds the sales proposal tool: visual proposals with images and side-by-side comparisons. The card lists that tool as a "$40/mo value" included on MAX. A cleanup or a planting that has to be chosen as two options on one designed proposal is the MAX feature. A single estimate with a price-book line is Basic.

**Invoicing.** Basic includes estimates and invoicing, and the customer can pay from the invoice. The [QuickBooks sync article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6293215-quickbooks-online-syncing-information-from-housecall-pro) describes deposit invoices and partial invoices moving to QuickBooks. It does not say which plan can create them. The lawn page says reminders and auto-charge of a card on file keep route billing on schedule. The plan for that auto-charge is in the payments note.

**Payments.** Rates are from Housecall Pro's [payments article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2046930-housecall-pro-payment-processing-options/), dated July 20, 2026, and the pricing FAQ.

- Card swiped, tapped, chipped, or taken with Tap to Pay: 2.59%.
- Tap to Pay also costs $5 per active device in any month that device takes a Tap to Pay payment.
- Card entered by the customer on an invoice: 2.99%.
- Card typed by staff, scanned in the app, or saved on file: 3.49%.
- American Express, and commercial, corporate, or business cards, any method: 3.49%.
- ACH: 1%.
- PayPal and Venmo on an invoice: 2.99%. PayPal Pay Later and Klarna: 4.99%.
- Consumer financing: starting at 3.9%. The Basic card lists payments and consumer financing. The approval rules were not on the pages checked.
- Mobile check deposit: the pricing FAQ says depositing a check in the app has no fee. The payments article lists a 1–4 business day payout and no percentage for that method.
- Instant payout (Instapay) adds 1%.

[Automatic payments](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9944421-card-on-file-automatic-payments) charge a card on file either when the job is marked finished or on the invoice due date. The customer gets a receipt. The invoice itself is still sent by hand if you want them to see it. The article says the due-date trigger does not work for batch invoicing, and a charge that meets the rule is attempted for the full amount at that moment. It does not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX. The saved-card rate above is 3.49%, which is the rate a route would pay if this is how the weekly visits are collected.

**Chemical and treatment records.** The pages checked do not describe a pesticide log with product, concentration, applicator license, weather, and a CSV export. [Job inputs](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6973952-job-inputs-and-flat-rate-services) can record materials and miscellaneous costs on a job. Those lines stay off the customer invoice and count as job cost. The recurring-job article says job inputs do not carry onto the next visit, so a fertilizer line entered on this week's mow is not waiting on next week's. [Custom fields](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/361372-custom-fields) can hold a note you define, and that article says they are not supported on recurring-job setup, so each visit is filled in on its own. That is a note, not the chemical record Jobber documents.

**QuickBooks.** The Essentials card includes QuickBooks Online sync and describes it as an import of existing QuickBooks data. MAX includes Essentials. After the connection, the [sync article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6293215-quickbooks-online-syncing-information-from-housecall-pro), dated September 3, 2026, says the integration typically runs one way, Housecall Pro to QuickBooks Online: invoices, payments (including service-plan payments), new customers, and new price-book items. An invoice edit in Housecall Pro can still need a manual push before QuickBooks updates. Estimates do not sync. A script embedded on the pricing page lists "QuickBooks online and desktop" under Essentials. The card itself says QuickBooks Online. See unverified claims.

**Mobile app.** Every plan includes the iOS and Android app, in English and Spanish, and the pricing page lists offline viewing on every plan. The lawn page says crews can see the day, update the job, and take payment in the app. Recurring jobs can be created from the app. The chemical-style record, if you are keeping one in Jobber instead, is the item that Jobber keeps off the phone.

### What a crew of this size actually compares

For one person, the shelf prices are Jobber Core at $49 month-to-month or $29 billed annually, and Housecall Pro Basic at $79 month-to-month or $59 billed annually. Core is the lower subscription. Basic is the entry plan that includes job costing.

For five people on a mowing route, the shelf prices are Jobber Connect at $199 month-to-month or $149 billed annually, and Housecall Pro Essentials at $189 month-to-month or $149 billed annually. The feature split at that price is in the quick answer: Connect names driving-distance route optimization and automatic pay on recurring jobs. Essentials names grouped routes, phone GPS, flat-rate pricing, and job costing. Live-traffic reordering is Housecall Pro MAX, at $329 month-to-month or $299 billed annually for eight users.

For five people selling larger landscape quotes, Jobber Grow is $299 month-to-month or $229 billed annually. Housecall Pro MAX, the plan with the visual proposal, is $329 or $299 on those same labels, with eight users included.

## What to watch out for

A user is someone who opens the schedule. Jobber's pricing page says that directly. A helper who rides along and never logs in is not one of those users. A tech who needs the day's list on a phone is. Core and Housecall Pro Basic are both one user. Five people in five trucks, each with the app, is a five-user plan.

Build the weekly mow as a recurring job. In Jobber, create it that way at the start. A one-off job will not convert later, and route optimization only reorders visits that have no clock time. In Housecall Pro, a weekly series is a recurring job, and a service plan stops at 12 visits a year. Buying MAX to "get recurring" buys the membership tool. The recurring-job articles never call that tool MAX.

Jobber's route reorder is an admin action on the website, for up to seven days of anytime visits. It is not a button in the mower's pocket. Housecall Pro's Essentials "Routes" line and its MAX "Route optimization" line are different sentences on the pricing card.

Count the card rate you will actually pay. A mowing route charged to a saved card is, on the pages checked, Jobber's published card rate of 2.9% + 30¢ if automatic payments use that rate, and Housecall Pro's saved-card rate of 3.49%. The in-person 2.59% rate is a different method. Tap to Pay on Housecall Pro adds $5 for each phone that takes one of those payments in a month.

Jobber's $149 five-user Connect price is a one-year term. The no-commitment price is $199. Housecall Pro's $149 Essentials price is the annual figure on the card. The month-to-month figure next to it is $189, and the FAQ says there is no long-term contract. Those are different kinds of commitment even when the dollar amount matches.

QuickBooks will drift if the same customers are edited in both products. Jobber's current Online sync is one way, out of Jobber, and Jobber says that version is still reaching select accounts. Jobber does not sync QuickBooks Desktop. Housecall Pro's Online article also describes a feed that typically runs one way, into QuickBooks, with manual pushes when an invoice changes.

Chemical records in Jobber are entered on the website by someone in the office, on a plan the help article does not name. Housecall Pro's material line on a recurring visit does not copy forward. A state that wants a pesticide log is a question to settle in a trial, on the plan you would actually buy.

Both sites lead with results: hours saved, revenue growth. Those are the vendors' aggregates. Nothing on this page comes from our using either app on a route.

## FAQ

**I mow alone. What is the least I can pay and still invoice the route?**

Jobber Core, if one login is enough: $29 per month billed annually, or $49 with no commitment. That includes scheduling, recurring jobs, quotes, invoices, online booking, and card payments. It does not include the route optimizer, QuickBooks Online, or automatic charging of repeat visits. Housecall Pro Basic is $59 per month billed annually, or $79 month-to-month, for one user, or $39.50 for the first three months against the $79. Basic includes job costing. The recurring-job article does not say Basic includes recurring jobs.

**Five of us need the app, and the work is weekly mowing. What bill should we expect?**

Jobber Connect for five users is $199 month-to-month or $149 billed annually, with a one-year term on the annual price. That plan's article lists routing and automatic payments. Housecall Pro Essentials is $189 month-to-month or $149 billed annually, with five users included, or $94.50 for three months against the $189. Essentials groups and sequences the day. The live-traffic reorder is MAX, at $299 billed annually for eight users. A sixth Connect user is where Jobber's help article and pricing page disagree. See unverified claims.

**Can the route charge the card without a phone call after each mow?**

Jobber names that on Connect, for recurring jobs only, using a card or bank account saved through Jobber Payments. The pricing page points at the published card rates and does not list a separate saved-card rate. Housecall Pro documents an automatic charge of a card on file when a job is finished or when the invoice is due, at the saved-card rate of 3.49%. The article does not name the plan. A Housecall Pro service plan also charges a saved card, and that membership is on MAX, with at most 12 visits a year.

**A storm wipes out Thursday. What does each product let us move?**

Jobber's route-optimization article documents selecting many appointments and moving them together. Only anytime visits are included when you then re-optimize the order. Housecall Pro's recurring-job article lets you change one visit or that visit and every future visit in the series, including the date and the assigned person. The lawn page describes dragging visits when the day changes and does not name a plan.

**We spray fertilizer and the state wants a record. Which plan has it?**

Jobber's chemical-tracking article describes that record and says it is on select plans, entered on the website. It does not name Core, Connect, Grow, or Plus. Housecall Pro's pages checked for this guide do not describe that log. Materials can be entered on a job as a cost, and they do not copy to the next recurring visit.

**We sell patios and plantings, not just mowing. Who has the bigger quote?**

Jobber Grow adds optional line items, images, and markups on the quote. Five users are $229 billed annually or $299 month-to-month. Payment schedules, a deposit and later invoices on a one-off job, are on every Jobber plan. Wisetack, at 3.9%, can sit on a one-off residential quote if Wisetack approves the company. It does not cover a repeating mow. Housecall Pro's side-by-side visual proposal is on MAX, eight users at $299 billed annually or $329 month-to-month. Estimates start on Basic, which also lists consumer financing from 3.9%.
