A small plumbing company is usually buying software for a week that mixes a burst pipe, a water-heater quote with a couple of options, a drain or water-heater agreement that should bill itself, and the parts that went on the job. This page compares [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) and [Housecall Pro's plumbing package](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/plumbing-software/) on that work. It is a comparison of the vendors' pricing pages and help centers, checked on November 21, 2026. We have not run either product on a job.

## Who it's for

Use this if you run residential or light-commercial plumbing with one person or a shop of up to about ten, and the owner still answers the phone or builds the day. That includes a solo plumber who quotes in the driveway, and a shop where each tech needs the schedule on a phone.

It is not a comparison with enterprise plumbing systems, and it is not Housecall Pro's generic plan or its HVAC package. Plumbing has its own Housecall Pro package. On that package, memberships and the visual proposal sit on Essentials. On the generic pricing page, those two sit on MAX, and job costing sits on Basic. The plumbing cards are the ones priced below.

Jobber Plus and Housecall Pro plumbing MAX are in the pricing section so the after-hours and live-traffic lines have a price. A shop of this size can ignore them unless it needs the included receptionist, a pipeline, or live-traffic reordering.

## Quick answer

Prices below are the ones to budget. The pricing section has the date, the billing term, and the page each number came from.

**One login.** [Jobber Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan: **$29 per month billed annually**, or **$49 month-to-month**. Core includes the calendar, online booking, quotes, invoices, recurring jobs, and card payments. Wisetack financing, at 3.9% to the contractor, can sit on a one-off residential quote on Core if Wisetack approves the company. The Core article says on-my-way texts and optional line items are not on Core. Job costing, QuickBooks Online, and automatic charging of a saved card are named on higher plans. Answering the phone after hours is the Receptionist add-on at **$99 per month** on Core, with 100 conversations in the billing cycle and $0.79 after that.

Housecall Pro plumbing Basic is the one-user card: **$79 per month**, or **$39.50 for the first three months** printed against that $79. The annual price fields on the plumbing page were the placeholder `$XX`, so this guide does not state a separate annual price. Basic includes scheduling and dispatch, estimates, a price book, payments, consumer financing, fee-free card surcharging, a plumbing KPI dashboard, and purchase orders. The plumbing card does not list flat-rate pricing, job costing, memberships, or the visual proposal. Those are on plumbing Essentials.

Buy Core if one person needs the day on a calendar and a lower ongoing bill. Buy plumbing Basic if a price book, purchase orders, and fee-free surcharging matter on the entry plan. Buy plumbing Essentials at **$229 per month** for one user, or **$114.50 for three months**, if that person prices from flat-rate labor and material markups and wants job costing on the same plan. Jobber's step for optional quote choices, markups, and job costing is Grow: one user at **$149 per month billed annually**, or **$199 month-to-month**. Grow still stores a maintenance agreement as a recurring job. It does not sell a membership on the quote.

**About five people, each with the app, selling replacements and agreements.** Jobber Connect for five users is **$149 per month billed annually**, or **$199 month-to-month**. The Connect article names routing, QuickBooks Online, and automatic payments on recurring jobs. Jobber Grow for five users is **$229 billed annually**, or **$299 month-to-month**. Grow names optional line items, markups, job costing, and Find a Time.

Housecall Pro plumbing Essentials, on the team card, is **$299 per month** with five users included and **$100 per month** for each user after that, or **$149.50 for three months** against the $299. That card lists the sales proposal, memberships sold on the estimate or the job, equipment history, flat-rate pricing, price-book commissions, job costing, and QuickBooks Online. Live-traffic route optimization and pricing insights are on plumbing MAX. The MAX team card is **$649** with eight users, then **$75** each, or **$324.50 for three months**.

Essentials is the plan that matches if the agreement is sold on the water-heater proposal and the price comes from a flat-rate book. Grow is the plan that matches if a quote with choices and a recurring job are enough, and the shop will stay the year for the $229 price. The $299 Essentials figure and Grow's $299 month-to-month figure are the same dollars under different terms. Jobber's annual and one-year prices are a one-year term. Housecall Pro's pricing FAQ says its plans do not require a long-term contract. The plumbing page did not print an annual dollar amount.

**Ten logins.** Jobber lists a 10-user Grow pack at **$299 billed annually**, or **$399 month-to-month**. The Grow article says the plan supports up to 10 users. Jobber also lists a 10-user Connect pack at **$229 billed annually**. The Connect article, updated November 18, 2026, says Connect supports up to five users. See unverified claims.

Housecall Pro does not print a 10-user plumbing pack. From the team cards, Essentials is five users at $299 plus five more at $100, which is **$799**. MAX is eight users at $649 plus two more at $75, which is also **$799**. Those are calculations from the printed rates, not a bundle the page advertises. The annual fields were still `$XX`.

**The 2 a.m. call is a separate bill, except on Jobber Plus.** Plus for five users is **$399 per month billed annually**, or **$499 month-to-month**, and the Receptionist article says Plus includes Receptionist with unlimited conversations. Housecall Pro's help center calls CSR AI an optional add-on and does not publish its price. The phone system that can forward a closed-hours call to an on-call cell starts at **$45 per month** for call tracking, or **$65 per month** for VoIP plus call tracking, plus minute overages.

## Jobber

Jobber's plumbing page describes scheduling, quotes, invoicing, financing, and a client portal in one account. The plan articles say which of those a shop of this size actually gets. [Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) is the one-person plan. [Connect](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-connect-plan/) is where routing, QuickBooks Online, and automatic payments are named. [Grow](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-grow-plan/) is where a quote can carry choices and where the plan article describes job costing. [Plus](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-plus-plan/) is where Receptionist is included and where profit alerts are named.

**Scheduling and dispatch.** Core includes requests, online booking, the calendar, and jobs. A job is one-off or recurring. A one-off job can be a single visit or up to 20 visits on different days, without a repeating schedule. That is the documented shape of a multi-day repipe. A drain agreement that repeats is a recurring job, and [job basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-basics/) says the type cannot be switched later.

[Route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/route-optimization-new-schedule/), updated November 13, 2026, is on Connect, Grow, and Plus. An admin reorders the day on Jobber.com. The app then shows the new order. Only anytime visits, the ones with no start and end time, are reordered. A call you put on the calendar at 2 p.m. stays put. Optimization uses driving distance, can cover one person or the whole team, and can run up to seven days at a time. The same article documents selecting many appointments and moving or reassigning them together.

The Connect plan article also says the plan can route the day's work and assign an incoming job to the closest person on the map. That sentence is broader than the anytime-visit optimizer. This guide does not treat them as the same button. Connect also lists live vehicle GPS through FleetSharp, including speed, direction, fuel, and mileage on the map in Jobber. The Connect article does not say the hardware is included in the subscription.

[Find a Time](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/new-schedule/), on Grow and Plus, is the tool that places a timed visit. Open slots account for the length of the appointment and drive time between the previous and next jobs. Drive time uses turn-by-turn routes and historical traffic. The schedule does not add drive time from home or the shop on the first or last appointment of the day. Suggested slots and assignees with the shortest drive show in green. A tech who is still at home for the first call of the morning is outside that calculation.

On-my-way texts are sent from the visit in the app, with a chosen arrival window from 5 to 60 minutes. The [article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/on-my-way-text-messages-in-the-jobber-app/), updated November 12, 2026, says the feature is on select plans. The Core article says it is not on Core. The plan that adds it is not named. Two-way texting, so the customer can reply in Jobber, is named on Grow.

**After-hours answering.** [Receptionist](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/receptionistpowered-by-jobber-ai/) is the documented after-hours tool. It answers calls and texts on a dedicated Jobber number in the United States, Canada, or the United Kingdom. An existing business number can be forwarded to it. It can answer questions, take a request, and book a job on the calendar, and the account controls which of those it may do. Plus includes it with unlimited conversations. The Plus plan article still heads that section "AI Receptionist Beta Access." On Core, Connect, and Grow the add-on is $99 per month, with 100 conversations in each billing cycle and $0.79 for each conversation after that. Conversations tagged "Spam" or "No conversation" do not count. The pricing page lists the same $99 add-on. Add-ons are not available during the trial.

**Quotes.** Core quotes use your branding, there is no cap on how many you send, and the customer approves them in client hub. Optional line items are not on Core. Grow adds them: add-ons, or packages the customer picks between, plus images and a markup that shows an estimated margin. That is the plumbing version of a water-heater repair, a like-for-like replacement, and a larger unit on one quote. The [optional-line-item article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/optional-line-items-on-quotes/) says the customer selects lines in client hub and the total updates. Unselected lines do not move onto the job. Line items come from the products and services list. Connect follows up on a quiet quote on a preset schedule. Grow can change the timing of those follow-ups.

**Price list and parts.** The [products and services list](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/products-services-list/) is the saved price list: name, description, unit price, and whether the item is a product or a service. Unit cost, markup, and a photo are on select plans. The Grow article is the plan article that names markups. A job can hold up to 100 line items. Nothing in that list article documents a quantity on the truck or in the shop.

The [Home Depot catalog](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-the-home-depot-integration/) is available in the United States on Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus. From a quote, a search returns Home Depot's price and online stock, using stores within 50 miles of the company address. Stock for the nearest 20 of those stores can be opened from the line, and Jobber says that stock refreshes once every 24 hours. The line can take a markup. The article says the integration cannot be set up on the legacy QuickBooks integration. This is a supplier catalog at quote time. It is not a count of what is already on the truck.

**Invoicing.** On Core, an invoice is created from the job and the customer can pay it online. [Payment schedules](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/progress-invoicing/), updated November 12, 2026, are on every plan. They split a one-off job into a deposit and later invoices, by percent or by dollar amount, from the quote or from the job. The article says to set the schedule on a one-off job, not a recurring one. A repipe that bills at start and at the end fits. A yearly flush agreement does not use this tool. Issued progress invoices lock. Later changes are made on the job, then on the remaining payments.

**Payments and financing.** Jobber Payments is on Core in the US, Canada, and the UK. The [pricing table](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) shows the same three rates on Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus: cards at 2.9% + 30¢, Tap to Pay on the phone at 2.7% + 30¢, and US bank payments (ACH) at 1%. The Core article says instant payouts to a debit card are available for an extra 1%. Customers can add a tip when they pay online.

[Automatic payments](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automatic-payments/) charge a card or ACH account saved through Jobber Payments when a recurring job's invoice is created. The Connect article names that feature. The automatic-payments article says select plans. The pricing page says credit and debit card processing rates apply, and it does not publish a second rate for a card on file. The charge follows the job's billing schedule: after each completed visit, on the last day of the month, on a custom schedule, or when the job is closed. "As needed" does not work with automatic payments. A failed charge is attempted once.

Consumer financing is [Wisetack](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-wisetack-consumer-financing-integration/), a flat 3.9% to the contractor, on every plan except Lite, United States only. The quote has to be a one-off residential job, in a person's name, titled, between $500 and $65,000, and the work has to be finished within 90 days. The article says some businesses start with a financing limit below $65,000. A repeating schedule is excluded, so the flush agreement is out and a one-off water heater can be in. The industries that article lists as ineligible are pool and spa, residential and commercial cleaning, window washing, and snow removal. Plumbing is not on that list. Wisetack still has to approve the company. The article asks for a review history, 10 or more reviews averaging near 4.5 stars, and more than $12,500 a month or $150,000 a year. A quote in a company name, which is the usual commercial property-manager record, does not get the offer. If the quote is turned into more than one job, the article says the Wisetack payment applies to the first invoice. It recommends converting a financed quote into one job. If the finished invoice is higher than the financed quote, the customer pays the difference. Funds arrive 1–3 business days after the customer confirms the work is done.

**Service agreements.** Jobber's version of a flush or inspection agreement is a [recurring job](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/create-a-recurring-job/), and the Core workflow includes recurring jobs. Visits repeat weekly, biweekly, monthly, or on a custom pattern, including the first and third Friday. Billing is per visit, or a fixed price for the period, such as one amount per month. Jobber's own guidance is to end recurring jobs within a few years, because a very long schedule creates enough visits to slow the account down. A single visit can carry different line items, and those lines transfer to a per-visit invoice. On a fixed-price job they do not transfer. On Connect and above, automatic payments can charge the saved card or bank account when that invoice is created. There is no membership the customer accepts on the replacement quote with the same signature.

**Job costing.** The [job costing article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-costing/), updated November 12, 2026, says costing is on Grow and Plus and covers one-off and recurring jobs. Profit uses labor from timesheets, line-item unit costs, and expenses logged on the job. The article says to put material costs on product lines and labor on timesheets, because the same cost in both places is counted twice. On a recurring job the math uses the last 30 days, not the life of the agreement, and job-cost reporting is not available for recurring jobs. The Grow plan article, updated November 18, 2026, describes job costing on one-off jobs. Those two pages disagree. See unverified claims. Profit alerts, which flag a one-off job under a margin you set, are Plus. The costing article says they do not include recurring jobs. Plus also scans a supplier invoice into an expense and matches it to a job from the purchase-order number, after someone approves the draft.

**QuickBooks.** Connect includes QuickBooks Online. The [current items article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/how-items-sync-between-jobber-and-quickbooks-online-new-quickbooks-integration/), updated October 29, 2026, says the new sync is rolling out to select accounts. It runs one way, Jobber to QuickBooks Online. Clients, products and services, invoices, and payments sync. Paid status no longer comes back from QuickBooks into Jobber. Products from Jobber are created in QuickBooks as non-inventory products. The article says that item can be edited to Inventory in QuickBooks. It does not say Jobber reduces quantity on hand. The first connection can import clients and products from QuickBooks once, including unit cost when the product is new to Jobber. After that, edit those records in Jobber. Supported US versions named in the [integration FAQ](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quickbooks-integration-faqs-new-quickbooks-integration/) are Simple Start, Essentials, Plus, and Advanced. The pages read for this sync document QuickBooks Online. They do not walk through Desktop.

**Mobile app.** The iOS and Android apps are how the Core user, or each paid user on a higher plan, sees the day and takes payment. Jobber's pricing FAQ says the mobile app is available in Spanish for team members, and not for administrators or the account owner. Route optimization stays on the website. Recurring jobs can be created in the app.

## Housecall Pro

For this shop the product is the [plumbing package](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/plumbing-software/), not the generic Housecall Pro plan. The [package overview](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/15936858-plumbing-package-overview), dated September 1, 2026, says you get a standard Housecall Pro plan plus plumbing tools, and that memberships and the sales proposal tool come with Essentials on this package. The generic pricing page puts both of those on MAX, and it puts job costing on Basic. The plumbing Essentials card is the one that lists job costing. Confirm the plumbing package before you pay.

**Scheduling and dispatch.** Plumbing Basic includes scheduling, dispatch, and online booking, including booking from Google or the company website. That covers a one-person calendar and assigning the job to a tech. The plumbing page says a crew can be routed so the closest plumber takes a burst pipe, that the crew is visible on a live map, and that "On my way" texts go out with GPS. Those sentences do not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX. The plan card puts route optimization, "reorder jobs in one click based on real drive time and live traffic," on MAX.

The generic Essentials card lists a different routing line, "group and sequence jobs to cut drive time," and employee GPS from the phone, with no hardware. The plumbing Essentials card does not repeat those two lines. The package overview says the plumbing package includes the standard plan. See unverified claims.

[Vehicle GPS hardware](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6060351-vehicle-gps-tracking-overview) is a separate add-on at $20 per vehicle per month, with no contract. The live map in that article is the hardware map. The same article says the customer's on-my-way text can include a link to the driver's location, the vehicle, and the plate, and that the link expires when the job is started or finished, or after two hours. Trucks without a device do not add vehicle data to that link. The [notification settings](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/14171435-customer-notifications-in-settings) include an on-my-way message. That article does not name a plan.

**After-hours calls.** [CSR AI](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9740104-csr-ai-overview) is the help center's after-hours product. The AI Team article says the other AI teammates are included, and that CSR AI is an optional add-on. The CSR AI article does not publish a price. It says you sign up or contact sales. After hours, the settings are to call you, text you, or log the call. Emergency calls, during or after hours, can be forwarded to a number you choose or can send a text. The article says emergency handling stays on. One section of that article, dated July 9, 2026, still says booking jobs is coming soon. Another section of the same article describes a demo that books into the calendar. See unverified claims.

[Voice](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6666356-voice-faqs) is a separate phone system, not an answering service. The FAQ, dated September 28, 2026, says call tracking starts at $45 per month plus overages, with packages from 500 to 2,500 minutes, and VoIP plus call tracking starts at $65 per month plus overages, with packages from 500 to 6,000 minutes. A VoIP plan with 6,000 minutes is $350 per month. Minutes include inbound, outbound, voicemail, and forwarded calls. A voicemail is a flat one minute. The FAQ does not name Basic, Essentials, or MAX.

[Call flows](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6750234-voice-settings-overview) can follow company hours, custom hours, or run all day. A closed-hours path can forward to an employee or to an outside number, including a cell phone. The call-menu example in that article is a plumbing shop: press 2 for an emergency and the call goes to the on-call technician. The menu allows up to five options, one menu for open hours and one for closed hours. The Voice FAQ says this is the phone system. Live agents are [HCP Assist](https://www.housecallpro.com/hcp-assist/), and the pages checked do not publish a single Assist price. Coverage can be after hours, weekends, or all day, and the price depends on that coverage.

**Quotes.** Basic sends estimates. Essentials adds the sales proposal tool: visual proposals with images and side-by-side comparisons. On the plumbing page, Housecall Pro describes that tool as good, better, and best options on water heaters and repipes, and says a membership can be added on the same proposal. The generic pricing page lists the same tool on MAX and calls it a "$40/mo value."

**Flat-rate price book.** Basic includes a price book. The [price-book article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9778163-how-to-use-price-book-by-housecall-pro) says plumbing, HVAC, and electrical get a deeper default book than other industries, with homeowner-facing descriptions, and that you still enter your own prices. [Materials](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/5456326-price-book-materials-guide) can store a name, description, and part number.

Flat-rate pricing is on the plumbing Essentials card: "Include profit margins in your service prices." The [flat-rate article](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/5461788-flat-rate-pricing-overview), dated July 9, 2026, is the setup. Material markups are tiers by cost range. Labor rates take an hourly cost and an hourly price, and one rate can be the default. A service can then calculate a price from labor hours, materials, and the service, and show markup and gross margin. ## What to watch out for

A user is someone who opens the schedule. Jobber's pricing page says that directly. A helper who rides along and never logs in is not one of those users. A tech who needs the day's list on a phone is. Core and plumbing Basic are both one user. Five people in five trucks, each with the app, is a five-user plan. Ten Housecall Pro logins are not the five-user price.

Buy the plumbing package on purpose. On the generic pricing page, recurring service plans and the sales proposal tool are MAX features, and job costing is on Basic. On the plumbing package, memberships, the proposal, flat-rate pricing, and job costing are Essentials features, and Basic adds purchase orders, the KPI dashboard, and fee-free surcharging. The logo is the same. The price on the plumbing team card is not the generic Essentials price.

Build the agreement in the shape the product stores. In Jobber, create a recurring job at the start. A one-off repair will not convert later, and a payment schedule belongs on the one-off repipe, not on the agreement. In Housecall Pro, set the billing frequency before the customer accepts, keep included visits inside 12 a year, and expect a saved-card charge at 3.49% unless a surcharge is allowed where you work.

Count the card rate you will actually pay. A membership or a Jobber agreement charged to a saved card is, on the pages checked, Housecall Pro's saved-card rate of 3.49%, and Jobber's published card rate of 2.9% + 30¢ if automatic payments use that rate. The in-person 2.59% rate is a different method. Tap to Pay on Housecall Pro adds $5 for each phone that takes one of those payments in a month. Wisetack at 3.9% is a different product from the card rate, and it does not cover a repeating agreement or a quote in a company name.

A flat-rate book is not the same object in the two products. Housecall Pro Essentials documents labor rates, material markup tiers, and a calculated service price, edited on the website. Jobber Grow documents a markup and an estimated margin on quote lines. Profit Rhino is another $199 a month if the shop wants the prebuilt plumbing and drain tasks. The permit line in that book is a charge on the price list.

Neither product, on the pages checked, keeps a quantity of fittings on the truck by itself. Jobber can show Home Depot's store stock at quote time and can send parts to QuickBooks as non-inventory items. Housecall Pro can write a purchase order, record where a part was bought, and send the invoice line to QuickBooks. A running on-hand count is QuickBooks or a separate inventory app.

Jobber's route reorder is an admin action on the website, for anytime visits. It does not move a visit that already has a clock time. Find a Time, on Grow and Plus, is the drive-time tool for a timed call, and it skips the drive from home on the first call of the day. Housecall Pro's live-traffic reorder is on the plumbing MAX card. The plumbing page's closest-plumber sentence does not name a plan.

QuickBooks will drift if the same customers are edited in both products. Jobber's current Online sync is one way, out of Jobber, and Jobber says that version is still reaching select accounts. Housecall Pro's Online article also describes a feed that typically runs one way, into QuickBooks, with a manual push when an invoice's lines change. Estimates do not sync from Housecall Pro.

Both sites lead with results: hours saved, revenue growth. Those are the vendors' aggregates. Nothing on this page comes from our using either app on a job.

## FAQ

**I am the only plumber. What is the least I can pay and still quote a water heater?**

Jobber Core, if one login is enough: $29 per month billed annually, or $49 with no commitment. That includes scheduling, quotes, invoices, recurring jobs, and card payments. It does not include optional line items, job costing, QuickBooks Online, or automatic charging of a repeat visit. Wisetack, at 3.9%, can sit on a one-off residential quote of at least $500 if Wisetack approves the company. Housecall Pro plumbing Basic is $79 per month for one user, or $39.50 for the first three months on the card checked November 21, 2026. Basic includes a price book, consumer financing from 3.9%, and purchase orders. Flat-rate pricing, job costing, and the side-by-side proposal are on plumbing Essentials, at $229 for one user.

**Five of us need the app, and we sell agreements on the replacement. What bill should we expect?**

Use a five-user plan. Jobber Grow is $299 month-to-month or $229 billed annually, with a one-year term on the annual price. That plan's article names optional line items, markups, and job costing. The agreement in Jobber is a recurring job, which starts on Core, with automatic charging named on Connect. Housecall Pro plumbing Essentials for a team is $299 with five users included, or $149.50 for three months on the card, and a sixth user is $100 more. Essentials is the plumbing plan that includes memberships and the visual proposal. The annual price on that page was `$XX`.

**A pipe bursts after hours. What does each product actually do?**

Jobber Receptionist can answer the call or the text and can book on the calendar. It is included on Plus. On Core, Connect, and Grow it is $99 per month for 100 conversations. Putting a clock time on the closest tech, using drive time, is Find a Time on Grow and Plus, and that calculation does not include the drive from home on the first call. Housecall Pro's CSR AI article describes an add-on that can forward an emergency to an on-call number or text you. It does not publish a price, and it disagrees with itself about booking. A Voice call flow can ring that on-call cell from the closed-hours menu. Call tracking starts at $45 per month, VoIP at $65, before overages. The plumbing card's live-traffic reorder is MAX.

**We price from a flat-rate book. Which plan has it?**

Housecall Pro plumbing Essentials lists flat-rate pricing. The setup article covers material markup tiers and labor rates, with cost and price edited on the website. Profit Rhino is a separate $199 per month book of plumbing and drain tasks, plus miscellaneous lines that include permits. Jobber does not document that labor-rate builder. Grow documents a markup and an estimated margin on each quote line, and the products and services list is the saved price list. The Home Depot catalog, on Core and up in the United States, can drop a current store price onto a quote.

**Can the customer finance a water heater?**

Yes in both, on the entry plan, with a published starting cost of 3.9% to the contractor. Jobber's Wisetack article is specific: United States, one-off residential work in a person's name, titled quote, $500 to $65,000, finished within 90 days, and Wisetack has to approve the company. A repeating agreement and a quote whose customer is a company are excluded. If the financed quote becomes more than one job, the payment applies to the first invoice. Housecall Pro lists consumer financing on plumbing Basic, starting at 3.9%, and says you are paid in full when the job is confirmed complete. The approval rules were not on the pages checked. Klarna and PayPal Pay Later on a Housecall Pro invoice are 4.99%.

**Do either of these keep a parts count, or track a permit?**

Not as a warehouse count, on the pages checked. Jobber's price list has no quantity-on-hand field in the article, and the current QuickBooks sync creates products as non-inventory items. Home Depot stock is the store's stock, refreshed daily. Housecall Pro's inventory article points at QuickBooks Online Plus for quantity on hand. Plumbing Basic can write a purchase order you update yourself, and material lines can record vendor and purchase status. The only permit language found is Profit Rhino's miscellaneous price-book items, which include a permit charge along with diagnostic and disposal fees. That is a line on a quote, not a permit workflow.
