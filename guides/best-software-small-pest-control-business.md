A small residential pest control company is usually buying software for a week of repeat stops: the monthly or quarterly treatment that should already be on the route, the agreement that should bill itself, the chemical record the state can ask for, the inspection note or wood-destroying-insect report, and the invoice the customer pays without a second trip to the office. This page compares [GorillaDesk](https://gorilladesk.com/pricing/), [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/pest-control-software/), [FieldRoutes](https://www.fieldroutes.com/company/pricing), [PestPac](https://www.pestpac.com/pricing), and [Briostack](https://www.briostack.com/additionalpricing/) on that work for an owner-operator or a company of up to about five technicians. It is a comparison of the vendors' pricing pages and help centers, checked on November 28, 2026. We have not run any of these products on a route.

## Who it's for

Use this if you run residential pest control, including general household pests, mosquito, or termite work sold to homeowners, with one person or up to about five technicians, and the owner still builds the routes. That includes a solo operator who treats and invoices from the truck, and a shop where each technician needs the day's stops on a phone.

It stays with that size. FieldRoutes, PestPac, and Briostack publish features a small shop can read and do not publish a subscription price. Their sections say so. A multi-branch commercial operation, a franchise rollup, and Housecall Pro's HVAC, plumbing, or electrical package are out of scope. The Housecall Pro prices below are the standard prices printed on the pest control page. This guide did not find a separate pest package with its own price card.

## Quick answer

Prices below are the ones to budget. The pricing section has the date, the billing term, and the page each number came from. Where a vendor does not print a subscription price, the sentence says that.

**One route, and the technician logs the chemical on the phone.** [GorillaDesk Basic](https://gorilladesk.com/pricing/) is **$49 per month** for the first schedule, paid monthly. The [plans article](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/431660-plans-pricing), written February 8, 2026, says Basic includes material tracking and reporting, invoices, estimates, and work orders, and that the mobile app has no extra charge. The [job-completion article](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/5624512-completing-a-job-on-desktop-and-mobile) says materials can be added from the phone. Stripe and Square payments are on the Basic card. Stripe and Square subscription billing, the charge that can bill monthly while the visits stay on their own schedule, is on Pro. Pro is **$99 per month** for the first schedule. The [document article](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/8425460-document-integration-and-e-sign), dated May 7, 2025, uses the NPMA-33 as the example form and says a state or government form is integrated for a Pro user at no additional charge, with a wait.

**The office will type the chemical record, and you want a published card rate.** [Jobber Connect](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-connect-plan/) is the plan that names automatic payments and QuickBooks Online. One user is **$99 per month billed annually**, or **$139 month-to-month**. Five users are **$149 billed annually**, or **$199 month-to-month**. The [chemical-tracking article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/chemical-tracking/), updated November 26, 2026, says the record is on Connect, Grow, and Plus, and that there is no chemical tracking in the mobile app. Chemicals are entered on jobber.com. [Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/), updated November 27, 2026, is the one-person plan at **$29 billed annually** or **$49 month-to-month**, and its workflow includes a recurring job. The chemical-tracking article does not list Core.

**The agreement is the product, and the chemical log lives somewhere else.** Housecall Pro's [pest control page](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/pest-control-software/) prints standard prices: Basic **$59 per month billed annually** or **$79 billed monthly**, one user; Essentials **$149 billed annually** or **$189 billed monthly**, five users; MAX **$299 billed annually** or **$329 billed monthly**, eight users. The [pricing page](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) puts recurring service plans on MAX. A [service-plan template](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/2369524-create-service-plan-templates), dated July 21, 2026, holds up to 12 included visits a year. A quarterly plan fits that field. A monthly plan uses all 12. The same pest page says checklists, photos, and notes document the visit and do not replace dedicated chemical-application tracking.

**A state report, a termite form, or Sentricon, and you will take a quote.** [FieldRoutes](https://www.fieldroutes.com/company/pricing) does not print a dollar amount. The monthly subscription is based on active customers. There is no free trial. The pest page names NPMA-33 access, California Branch 3 wood-destroying-organism reporting, Sentricon, and AutoPay. [PestPac](https://www.pestpac.com/pricing) does not print a dollar amount. Termite inspection is on every tier card. The [chemical-tracking page](https://www.pestpac.com/features/chemical-tracking-software) names California material reports, California Cal-Ag reports, and Arizona TARF reports, and it does not name Small Business, Professional, or Enterprise. [Briostack](https://www.briostack.com/additionalpricing/) does not print a subscription price. The [scheduling page](https://www.briostack.com/pest-control-software/scheduling-routing-software/) says the technician app reports chemical usage with or without a data connection, and that California accounts submit CalAg reports from the product. SMS, mail, and migration fees on the additional-pricing page are published and are not the subscription.

Buy GorillaDesk Basic if one schedule needs the route, the invoice, and a material record the technician can add on the phone, and a Stripe or Square charge is enough. Buy GorillaDesk Pro if that same shop wants the subscription charge or the NPMA-33. Buy Jobber Connect if the chemical record will be entered on the website and the shop wants the published Jobber card rate. Buy Housecall Pro MAX, at the pest page's price, if the service plan is the agreement you sell and a chemical-use report is not the reason for the software. Ask FieldRoutes, PestPac, or Briostack for a written quote when the requirement is a state application export or a termite workflow those three pages describe and the priced products do not.

## GorillaDesk

GorillaDesk's [pricing page](https://gorilladesk.com/pricing/) sells Basic, Pro, and Growth per schedule, not per login. A schedule is a technician who runs a route alone. Technicians who ride together share the crew leader's schedule. Unlimited admin, manager, sales, and crew users are on every plan. The trial is 14 days, with no credit card. Monthly plans have no setup fee and no contract. The February 8, 2026 [plans article](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/431660-plans-pricing) says a yearly prepay is eleven months' price. The pricing-page cards show the monthly amounts and do not print that annual total.

**Scheduling and the route.** The [recurring-jobs article](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/1089485-recurring-jobs), dated March 2, 2026, says a job can repeat daily, weekly, monthly, or annually, and that the next job is created when the current one is completed or canceled. Its example of three treatments 14 days apart is a daily repeat with an interval of 14 days, ending after three jobs. Months, or a weekday in the month, can be skipped. The article does not name Basic, Pro, or Growth. The compare page lists scheduling and dispatching on Basic, Pro, and Growth. A route that is not closed stays a recurrence tile, and the article says that tile cannot be worked until the current job is completed or canceled.

Basic routing on the pricing card is limited to 25 stops at a time. Pro adds Drive Matrix, which the card describes as real-time street routing and drive times. Growth adds the estimated drive time between jobs, best available time when a job is placed, and job magnet, which flags stops that should be pulled together.

**Agreements and payments.** Basic includes invoices, estimates, work orders, and what the card calls advanced invoice frequencies. Stripe and Square are on Basic. The pricing FAQ says the Square connection is the one that can use Square's hardware to swipe a card, the Stripe connection is the one that can run subscription billing, and both can be connected. GorillaDesk does not publish Stripe's or Square's rate.

Subscription billing is on the Pro card. The [compare page](https://gorilladesk.com/pricing/compare/) says Pro adds subscription billing, and its plan-difference table puts a dash on Basic for that row. The same page's "included in every plan" Stripe line says a company can store cards and set up subscription billing. Those two lines on one page do not say the same thing. See unverified claims. The [subscription article](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/8927541-stripe-and-square-subscription-billing) says a Pro account turns the feature on in Stripe or in Square, stores the customer's card, and then either charges that card or emails an invoice on the plan's interval. Each charge creates a new invoice. The subscription does not change older invoices or a balance outside the subscription. The plans article describes the same feature as the way to service on one rhythm and bill on another, including weekly service with a monthly bill.

**Chemical records.** The Basic card lists material tracking and reporting. The compare page lists material tracking on every plan and says the usage is recorded in the field. The plans article says the same thing is on Basic, and that the charges beyond the plan are a texting package and the phone integration. The [material article](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/1086001-material-usage), dated September 4, 2025, calls the tool an add-on you activate. The lists it documents are materials with an EPA number, locations, target pests, methods, units, area, and dilution. Recorded materials can print as a table on the invoice and the work order, or they can be hidden on either PDF. The report is under Reports, Material Use, with a summary and a detailed list, split between materials on the job and materials on a device. Presets, which narrow the methods and pests for one chemical, are Pro and Growth. The job-completion article says the phone can add a material from the same list.

Device tracking and barcoding are on the Pro card. That is the pricing-page line for traps and equipment, with trend reporting. The material article also describes a material line on a device. It does not repeat the plan name.

**Inspection forms.** Documents and e-signatures are on the Pro card. The compare page's Pro summary says Pro adds digital documents, and the plan-difference table describes them as a library of state-specific forms, with a dash on Basic. The Pro and Growth cells in that table did not render as checkmarks on the fetch used here. The document article says a state or government form is integrated for a Pro user at no additional charge, with a waiting time, and that a custom document goes through support and a development queue. The worked example is the NPMA-33: preview it, add it to the job, fill the highlighted fields, and send it for a signature. The phone has a mobile layout of the same form. From the app, a document already on the job can be sent for an e-signature by email or by SMS. After the customer signs, the job stores the status and the history. Signed documents for one customer sit on that account, and Reports has an All Documents list.

**QuickBooks and texting.** The plans article says the Pro QuickBooks Online integration pushes customers, invoices, and payments from GorillaDesk to QuickBooks Online. Texting is separate. The pricing page charges $5 per month for the local number, then credits: $50 for 1,000 messages, $100 for 2,500, or $200 for 8,000. Credits do not expire and are deducted for outgoing messages. The plans article says incoming messages are free. The same article prices the integrated phone service from $65 per month per user on its basic phone plan. That phone system is optional.

The pricing FAQ says a free import can bring contact information, service and billing address, notes, and a starting balance, and that complete past service or billing history cannot be imported. The plans article says a customer import usually takes two business days and a customer-plus-job import can take five, and that migration is free. See unverified claims.

## Jobber

Jobber's [pest control page](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/pest-control-software/) describes recurring visits, routing, chemical records, invoices, and the mobile app. The page metadata shows a modification date of October 30, 2026. It does not name Core, Connect, Grow, or Plus. The plan articles do. [Core](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/), updated November 27, 2026, is one user, with unlimited quotes, jobs, and invoices. [Connect](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-connect-plan/), updated the same day, is where automatic payments, routing, and QuickBooks Online are named. The Connect article says the plan supports up to five users. The pricing page also sells larger Connect packs. See unverified claims.

**Scheduling and the route.** Core's workflow is a request, a quote, a job, an invoice, and a payment. The job step is a recurring or a one-off schedule. The [recurring-job article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/create-a-recurring-job/), updated November 12, 2026, lists weekly, biweekly, monthly, or a custom schedule. The custom examples are two weekdays, or the first and third Friday. The article also says the custom schedule can be weekly, monthly, or annual. The pest page says the cadence can be monthly, quarterly, or seasonal. The help article does not use the word quarterly. Jobber's own guidance is to end a recurring job within a few years, because a very long schedule creates enough visits to slow the account down. Billing is per visit, or a fixed price for the period, such as one amount per month. A single visit can carry different line items. Those lines transfer to a per-visit invoice. On a fixed-price job they do not.

[Route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/route-optimization-new-schedule/), updated November 26, 2026, is on Connect, Grow, and Plus. An admin runs it on Jobber.com. Only anytime visits, the ones with no start and end time, are reordered. A stop you put on the calendar at 2 p.m. stays put. Optimization uses driving distance, can cover one person or the whole team, and can run up to seven days at a time. The app then shows the new order. The Connect article also says the plan can assign an incoming job to the closest person on the map, and it lists live vehicle GPS through FleetSharp, including speed, direction, fuel, and mileage. The article does not say the hardware is included.

**Agreements and payments.** The recurring job is Jobber's agreement. There is no separate membership the customer accepts on a quote. Automatic payments, on Connect, Grow, and Plus, charge a card or a bank account saved through Jobber Payments when the recurring job's invoice is created. The [article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automatic-payments/), updated November 26, 2026, says this is available in the United States and Canada, and in the United Kingdom for subscribed accounts. The charge follows the billing schedule: after each visit, on the last day of the month, on a custom schedule, or when the job is closed. The "as needed" option turns the charge off. A failed charge is attempted once. The invoice stays open.

The [pricing table](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) shows the same three rates on Core, Connect, Grow, and Plus: cards at 2.9% + 30¢, Tap to Pay at 2.7% + 30¢, and US bank payments (ACH) at 1%. The table does not publish a separate card-on-file rate. The automatic-payments line says credit and debit card processing rates apply. The Core article says instant payouts to a debit card add 1%. Jobber Payments on these pages is the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom.

**Chemical records and inspection notes.** The chemical-tracking article, updated November 26, 2026, limits the feature to Connect, Grow, and Plus and says there is no chemical tracking in the mobile app. Setup on the website holds company licensing information, applicator license numbers, the chemical list, and tank mixtures. A treatment is entered from the client page. The fields the article names are the service address, the job, the applicator, the date and time, the chemical name and concentration, application details, weather, and an extra notes area. The list can be sorted by date, client, or applicator, and downloaded as a CSV. The article says this is the record for jurisdictions that require chemical or pesticide use to be kept for up to seven years. It does not name a state form.

The pest page says technicians capture inspection details, photos, and a signature from the app and can send the completed checklist before they leave. The pricing page lists checklists as a Connect addition, in the sentence "Standardize job documentation with checklists." The Core article does not include them. The Core article also says on-my-way texts are not on Core.

**QuickBooks.** The Connect article names QuickBooks Online. The [integration FAQ](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quickbooks-integration-faqs-new-quickbooks-integration/), updated November 27, 2026, says the new sync is rolling out to select accounts. It runs one way, Jobber to QuickBooks Online. Clients, products and services, invoices, payments, and timesheets sync. Paid status no longer comes back from QuickBooks into Jobber. The first connection can import clients and products from QuickBooks once. After that, edit those records in Jobber. Supported US versions named in that FAQ are Simple Start, Essentials, Plus, and Advanced. The same FAQ says Jobber does not sync to QuickBooks Desktop. The pricing page titles one feature line "QuickBooks Online and Xero sync." The FAQ answer describes QuickBooks Online.

## Housecall Pro

## What to watch out for

Count the unit the vendor bills. GorillaDesk bills a schedule. A technician who never logs in and rides with the crew leader is not a second schedule. A technician who runs a route alone is. Five solo routes are five schedules, and Basic's fourth and fifth schedule do not match across GorillaDesk's own pages. Jobber bills a user, someone who opens the schedule. Core is one user in the plan article. Five people with five phones are the five-user Connect pack, at $149 billed annually or $199 month-to-month, not five times the one-user price if the pack is the lower figure. Housecall Pro Basic on the pest FAQ is one user. Essentials is five. The price of a second Basic user was not on the pages checked.

Close the GorillaDesk job. The recurring-jobs article says the next visit is created when the current job is completed or canceled. A month of tiles left in recurrence status is a month the next stop was not generated. Jobber's recurring article says to end the series within a few years so the visit list does not slow the account down. A one-off Jobber job does not become the agreement later. Create the recurring job as a recurring job.

Put the chemical record where the product actually stores it. GorillaDesk Basic documents a material line the technician can add on the phone, with an EPA number, and Pro adds presets and the device record. Jobber's chemical-tracking article says the record is entered on the website, on Connect and above, and that the app does not have it. A checklist on the Jobber phone is a different object. Housecall Pro's pest FAQ says the checklist and the photos do not replace a chemical-application record. PestPac's chemical page describes the state export and does not say it is inside Small Business. Briostack's scheduling page says the technician reports chemical usage in the app, including offline, and the subscription that includes that app is a quote.

A service plan is not a weekly route. Housecall Pro's template stops at 12 included visits a year. Quarterly pest control fits. Monthly pest control fills the field. A weekly or every-other-week program does not fit one template. The recurring job is the other Housecall Pro tool, and the pest page does not name its plan. GorillaDesk's subscription, on Pro, is the billing interval. The visit is still the recurring job. Jobber's automatic charge, on Connect, follows the recurring job's invoice schedule and tries a failed card once.

Count the card rate you will actually pay. A card tapped on the phone is Jobber's 2.7% + 30¢, or Housecall Pro's 2.59% plus $5 in a month that phone takes one of those payments. A saved card charged by the business is Housecall Pro's 3.49%. Jobber's pricing page points at the published card rate and does not list a second one. GorillaDesk, FieldRoutes, PestPac, and Briostack did not publish a card rate on the pages checked. GorillaDesk's rate is Stripe's or Square's. A Housecall Pro service plan that is not paid in cash uses that saved-card rate unless a different method is on the invoice.

QuickBooks will drift if the same customers are edited in both products. Jobber's current Online sync is one way, out of Jobber, and Jobber says that version is still reaching select accounts. Jobber does not sync QuickBooks Desktop. GorillaDesk's plans article describes a push of customers, invoices, and payments into QuickBooks Online on Pro. The import is not the history. GorillaDesk's pricing FAQ says the free import is contacts, addresses, notes, and a starting balance, and that complete past service or billing history is not imported.
